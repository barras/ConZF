\title{Definable regularity:\\
  \large towards a set model of $\CIC$ with $\omega$ universes and excluded middle in $\ZF$}
\author{Mario Carneiro}
% Drafted with Claude; adjust authorship/acknowledgement as you see fit.
\date{Draft, \today}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Calculus of Inductive Constructions with an impredicative universe
of propositions, a tower of predicative universes, and excluded middle,
but without a choice or description operator, is usually modelled in
$\ZFC$ with one inaccessible cardinal per universe. We ask whether $\ZF$
alone proves its consistency, and we set out a model that would show
it. The function spaces of the model contain only the functions
definable by a term from parameters of bounded content; the extent
$\lambda_N$ of the universe $\Type_N$ is an ordinal with a regularity
property for such definable families only; and truth in the model,
which is not available while the extents are being determined, is
handled by evaluating terms relative to an arbitrary oracle for the
class of admitted elements and instantiating the oracle with the model
itself at the end. We prove that the model is sound provided the
extents exist, and we reduce their existence to a single statement, a
rank lemma saying that the types a term can denote below the extent of
a universe are bounded independently of that extent. We prove what is
needed of it, by majorization in Howard's style, for the theory with two
universes in which $W$-types have small branching and accessibility is
taken on small carriers, which is the fragment that interprets Zermelo
set theory with recursion; the extent of the universe is an ordinal
$\alpha$ with $V_\alpha$ a $\Sigma_2$-elementary substructure of the universe,
elementarity doing the work that the regularity of an inaccessible does
in the usual model, without any assumption on cofinality. So, subject
to a soundness check that is written by cases, $\ZF$ proves the
consistency of that theory, whose strength therefore lies strictly
between Zermelo set theory with recursion and $\ZF$. The restriction
to small carriers turns out to be the boundary and not a limitation of
the method: a late section argues that accessibility on a big carrier
computes ordinals that are only specified by propositions, that this
gives Replacement in the constructible sets-as-trees, and hence that the
unrestricted theory with two universes already interprets $\ZFC$, so
that the question in the title has a negative answer for it and remains
open for small carriers with more universes. This is a working paper: it
fixes the type theory and the
definitions, records what is proved and what is not, and explains the
design decisions by the counterexamples that forced them.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{The question.}
Let $\CIC_\omega$ be the intensional type theory of
Section~\ref{sec:tt}: an impredicative, proof-irrelevant $\Prop$,
predicative cumulative universes $\Type_0:\Type_1:\cdots$, dependent
products and sums, $W$-types, natural numbers and finite types, an
identity type in $\Prop$ eliminating into every sort, propositional
truncation, quotients, an accessibility predicate eliminating into every
sort, propositional extensionality, and excluded middle in $\Prop$. It
has no choice operator, no description operator, and no unique choice.
Werner's set-theoretic model \cite{werner97} interprets $\Type_i$ as
$V_{\kappa_i}$ with $\kappa_i$ inaccessible, and with a description
operator the inaccessibles are necessary, since the sets-as-trees
interpretation then validates Replacement. Without one they are not
known to be. We conjecture:

\begin{conjecture}[main]\label{conj:main}
$\ZF\vdash\Con(\CIC_\omega+\EM)$.
\end{conjecture}

\emph{Note added.} Section~\ref{sec:bigacc} argues that this is false
as stated, because accessibility on a big carrier yields Replacement
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bigacc}), and that the conjecture to pursue is the one
for the theory with accessibility on carriers in $\Type_0$ only. The
introduction and Sections~\ref{sec:sem} to~\ref{sec:ranklemma} have not
been rewritten around this.

\paragraph{What is known.}
From below, $\CIC$ with $n+2$ universes and excluded middle interprets
$\ZFh_n$, Zermelo set theory with recursion terms and $n$ universes, and
proves its consistency \cite{paper2}; $\ZF$ proves the consistency of
all of these, so the lower bound is well below $\ZF$ and the boundary
is exactly Replacement for functional relations that are not given by
terms. From above, the inaccessibles cannot be removed from Werner's
model by replacing set-theoretic function spaces with realizability:
a set-sized universe of assemblies closed under sums over tracked
families has inaccessible size \cite[Prop.~4.4]{paper1}. The reason is
the context $X{:}\Type_0,\ f{:}X\to\Type_0$: whatever $f$ is allowed to
range over, $\Sigma x{:}X.\,f\,x$ must be in $\Type_0$, and the extent of
$\Type_0$ is a singular ordinal unless it is inaccessible, so some
small $X$ carries a cofinal family. No pointwise condition on $f$ can
exclude such a family (Example~\ref{ex:climb}). What excludes it in
$\ZFh$ is that a family there is a term, and the rank of a term's value
is bounded by an ordinal function of the ranks of its parameters, under
which the height of the model is closed \cite[\S3--4]{paper2}.

\paragraph{The model.}
We import that argument into type theory. The function spaces of the
model consist of the functions that are values of terms whose
parameters have bounded \emph{content}, the content of a parameter being
the supremum of the ranks of the small types occurring in it; and the
extent $\lambda_N$ of $\Type_N$ is \emph{$N$-closed}: as the parameters of
a term range over content below $\gamma<\lambda_N$, those values of the
term that are types of rank below $\lambda_N$ have rank below some
$\delta<\lambda_N$. This is what is left of the regularity of an
inaccessible when families are restricted to the definable ones, and
it makes the uniform half of type formation sound by definition. Two
difficulties remain.

The first is a circularity. The extents are determined from the bottom
up, and while $\lambda_N$ is being determined neither the higher
extents nor truth in the model are available: a proposition may
quantify over a function space, which is a set of definable functions,
whose definitions contain propositions. We resolve it by evaluating
terms relative to an \emph{oracle}, an arbitrary set $\X$ of admitted
elements: function spaces are cut down to $\X$, truth is computed from
the resulting decoding, and closedness quantifies over all oracles and
all higher extents. The generated elements $\A$ are the values of terms
under any oracle, and the model is the evaluation under the oracle
$\X:=\A$, which is one of those quantified over. No truth assignment
has to be guessed.

The second is the existence of $N$-closed ordinals. It follows from a
\emph{rank lemma} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank}): the types below the cut
that a term can denote are bounded by an ordinal that does not depend
on the cut. This is the analogue of the rank lemma of \cite{paper2} and
we believe it, having failed to refute it in several rounds; but its
proof is not an adaptation of Howard's majorizability argument
\cite{howard73}, for reasons given in Section~\ref{sec:ranklemma}, and
it is open in general. It is proved in Section~\ref{sec:proved} for
$\CIC_2$ without $W$-types and $\Acc$, where the types that are not small
have a simple kind fixed by their syntax and all bounds are countable,
and in Section~\ref{sec:cic2} for $\CIC_2$ with $W$-types of small
branching and $\Acc$ on small carriers, where recursions have Hartogs
length and the extent of $\Type_0$ is taken to be a $\Sigma_2$-correct
ordinal (Theorem~\ref{thm:cic2}). The second case contains the
interpretation of $\ZFh$ of \cite{paper2}, so it is the first instance
in which an upper bound below $\ZF$ meets the lower bound.
So the status of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} is: reduced to
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank}, modulo a soundness proof that is written by
cases and not yet checked rule by rule.

\paragraph{Why intensional.}
An earlier draft \cite[\S8]{paper2} worked with the extensional theory
of \cite{vdbergotten23,paper1}. Equality reflection makes the typing of
a term depend on the truth of the equations in its context, which
forced closedness to be stated for untyped terms, made every term
typable in some context, and defeated every attempt to stratify a
majorizability argument by types. The theory that proof assistants
implement is intensional, and for it none of this arises: typing is
decidable and independent of truth, and definitional equality is
computation, which holds in a set-theoretic evaluation outright. The
one place where a false equation could still do harm, the eliminator of
the identity type, is evaluated with a check.

\section{The type theory}\label{sec:tt}

\paragraph{Syntax.}
Sorts are $s::=\Prop\mid\Type_i$ for $i<\omega$. Terms:
\begin{align*}
t,u,A,B,C::={}&x\mid s\mid\Pi x{:}A.B\mid\lambda x{:}A.t\mid t\,u
  \mid\Sigma x{:}A.B\mid\langle t,u\rangle\mid\pi_1t\mid\pi_2t\\
 &\mid Wx{:}A.B\mid\mathsf{sup}\,t\,u\mid\ind^W_Ct
  \mid\mathbf n\mid\mathbf k_{\mathbf n}\mid\ind^{\mathbf n}_Ct_0\cdots t_{n-1}
  \mid\N\mid0\mid\mathsf S\,t\mid\ind^\N_Ct\,u\\
 &\mid t=_Au\mid\refl\,t\mid\ind^=_Ct
  \mid\|A\|\mid|t|\mid\ind^{\|\|}_Ct
  \mid A/R\mid[t]_R\mid\mathsf{lift}_Ct\,u\mid\mathsf{qind}_Pt\mid\mathsf{snd}_R\\
 &\mid\Acc_R\mid\mathsf{acc}\,t\,u\mid\ind^{\Acc}_Ct
  \mid\mathsf{propext}\mid\mathsf{em}
\end{align*}
with $\mathbf n$ the type with $n$ elements $\mathbf 0_{\mathbf n},\dots,(\mathbf{n{-}1})_{\mathbf n}$
for each $n<\omega$. We write $A\to B$ for $\Pi x{:}A.B$ with $x$ not
free in $B$, $\bot:=\|\mathbf 0\|$, $\neg P:=P\to\bot$, and
$P\vee Q:=\|\Sigma b{:}\mathbf 2.\,\ind^{\mathbf 2}_{\lambda\_.\Prop}\,P\,Q\,b\|$.

\paragraph{Judgments.}
$\vdash\Gamma$, $\Gamma\vdash t:A$, and $\Gamma\vdash t\equiv u:A$. We
write $\Gamma\vdash A:s$ for a type at a sort. The rules are those of a
pure type system with the following specifics.

\emph{Sorts and cumulativity.} $\Prop:\Type_0$ and $\Type_i:\Type_{i+1}$;
if $A:\Prop$ then $A:\Type_0$, and if $A:\Type_i$ then $A:\Type_{i+1}$.

\emph{Products.} If $A:s_1$ and $x{:}A\vdash B:s_2$ then
$\Pi x{:}A.B:\Prop$ when $s_2=\Prop$, and $\Pi x{:}A.B:\Type_{\max(i,j)}$
when $s_2=\Type_j$ and $s_1=\Type_i$, reading $\Prop$ as $\Type_0$ for
$s_1$. Abstraction and application as usual, with $\beta$ and $\eta$.

\emph{Sums, $W$-types, quotients.} If $A:\Type_i$ and
$x{:}A\vdash B:\Type_i$ then $\Sigma x{:}A.B:\Type_i$ and
$Wx{:}A.B:\Type_i$; if $A:\Type_i$ and $R:A\to A\to\Prop$ then
$A/R:\Type_i$. Pairs with projections, $\beta$ and $\eta$. Trees
$\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d:Wx{:}A.B$ for $a:A$ and $d:B[a/x]\to Wx{:}A.B$, with
$\ind^W_Cf:\Pi w{:}(Wx{:}A.B).\,C$ for a motive $w{:}W\vdash C:s$ in any
sort and
$f:\Pi a{:}A.\Pi d{:}(B[a/x]\to W).\,(\Pi b{:}B[a/x].\,C[d\,b/w])\to C[\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d/w]$,
and $\ind^W_Cf\,(\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d)\equiv f\,a\,d\,(\lambda b.\,\ind^W_Cf\,(d\,b))$.
Classes $[a]_R:A/R$ for $a:A$, the proof
$\mathsf{snd}_R:\Pi a\,a'{:}A.\,R\,a\,a'\to[a]_R=_{A/R}[a']_R$, and
$\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h:A/R\to C$ for $C:s$ in any sort, $f:A\to C$, and
$h:\Pi a\,a'{:}A.\,R\,a\,a'\to f\,a=_Cf\,a'$, with
$\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h\,[a]_R\equiv f\,a$; and the induction principle
$\mathsf{qind}_Pf:\Pi q{:}A/R.\,P\,q$ for $P:A/R\to\Prop$ and
$f:\Pi a{:}A.\,P\,[a]_R$.

\emph{Finite types and numbers.} $\mathbf n:\Type_0$ and $\N:\Type_0$ with
their constructors, and eliminators $\ind^{\mathbf n}_C\vec c:\Pi k{:}\mathbf n.\,C$
and $\ind^\N_Cc\,f:\Pi m{:}\N.\,C$ for motives in any sort, with the
usual computation rules.

\emph{Identity.} If $A:s$ and $a,a':A$ then $a=_Aa':\Prop$, with
$\refl\,a:a=_Aa$. For a motive $x{:}A,\,e{:}a=_Ax\vdash C:s$ in any sort
and $d:C[a/x,\refl\,a/e]$,
\[
\ind^=_Cd:\Pi x{:}A.\,\Pi e{:}a=_Ax.\,C,\qquad
\ind^=_Cd\,a\,(\refl\,a)\equiv d .
\]
There is no reflection rule.

\emph{Truncation.} If $A:s$ then $\|A\|:\Prop$, $|a|:\|A\|$ for $a:A$, and
$\ind^{\|\|}_Pf:\|A\|\to P$ for $P:\Prop$ and $f:A\to P$. It eliminates
into propositions only.

\emph{Accessibility.} For $X:s$ and $R:X\to X\to\Prop$,
$\Acc_R:X\to\Prop$, with
$\mathsf{acc}\,x\,h:\Acc_R\,x$ for $h:\Pi y{:}X.\,R\,y\,x\to\Acc_R\,y$. For a
motive $x{:}X\vdash C:s$ in any sort and
$f:\Pi x{:}X.\,(\Pi y{:}X.\,R\,y\,x\to C[y/x])\to C$,
\[
\ind^{\Acc}_Cf:\Pi x{:}X.\,\Acc_R\,x\to C,\qquad
\ind^{\Acc}_Cf\,x\,(\mathsf{acc}\,x\,h)\equiv f\,x\,(\lambda y\,r.\,\ind^{\Acc}_Cf\,y\,(h\,y\,r)).
\]

\emph{Proof irrelevance.} If $P:\Prop$ and $p,q:P$ then $p\equiv q:P$.

\emph{Axioms.} $\mathsf{propext}:\Pi P\,Q{:}\Prop.\,(P\to Q)\to(Q\to P)\to P=_\Prop Q$
and $\mathsf{em}:\Pi P{:}\Prop.\,P\vee\neg P$.

\emph{Conversion.} $\equiv$ is the congruence generated by the
computation rules above, $\beta$, $\eta$ for $\Pi$ and $\Sigma$, and
proof irrelevance; and if $t:A$ and $A\equiv B:s$ then $t:B$.

$\CIC_n$ is the theory with the sorts $\Prop,\Type_0,\dots,\Type_{n-1}$
only, and $\CIC_\omega$ is the union. Function extensionality is
derivable from the quotient rules by the usual argument. The theory is
close to the core of Lean~4 without $\mathsf{choice}$ \cite{carneiro19},
with $W$-types and $\Acc$ standing for the general inductive families,
and to the predicative calculus of inductive constructions with
$\mathsf{em}$, $\mathsf{propext}$ and quotients added. We have not
included general inductive families; we expect $W$, $=$ and $\Acc$ to
carry the features that matter for the strength, namely large
elimination over a well-founded structure of unbounded height and
singleton elimination.

\section{Evaluation relative to an oracle}\label{sec:sem}

All of this section is Werner's set-theoretic semantics \cite{werner97}
with three changes: universes have arbitrary ordinal extents; function
spaces, and the subtree functions of trees, are cut down to an arbitrary
set $\X$; and nothing is assumed to be sound. The evaluation is a total
$\ZF$-definable function, and what it is good for is decided afterwards.

\begin{definition}[structures, codes]\label{def:codes}
A \emph{structure} is an increasing sequence
$\vec\epsilon=(\epsilon_0<\epsilon_1<\cdots)$ of ordinals above $\omega$,
the \emph{extents}. We fix a representation of semantic objects as sets
in which natural numbers, the proof $\star$, pairs, functions, trees,
classes, and codes are pairwise distinct kinds, by tagging. The
\emph{codes} are: the constants $\bot,\top,\code\Prop,\code{\mathbf n},\code\N$,
of finite rank; the \emph{universe constants}
$\code{\U_i}:=\langle\mathsf U,\epsilon_i\rangle$, of rank about $\epsilon_i$;
and $\code\Pi(c,G)$, $\code\Sigma(c,G)$, $\code W(c,G)$, $\code/(c,r)$ for $c$
a code, $G$ a function into codes, the \emph{family}, and $r$ a function,
the \emph{relation}, in curried form. The truth
values are the codes $\bot$ and $\top$. Put $U_i:=\{c:c\text{ a code},\ \rk(c)<\epsilon_i\}$,
so that $\code{\U_i}\in U_{i+1}\setminus U_i$. A code is
\emph{hereditarily in} a set $\X$ if every family and relation occurring
in it, at any depth, belongs to $\X$.

Given $\lambda_0<\dots<\lambda_{N-1}$, an ordinal
$\alpha>\lambda_{N-1}$ (or $\alpha>\omega$ if $N=0$), and an increasing
sequence $\vec\alpha'$ of ordinals above $\alpha$, the
\emph{$(N,\alpha,\vec\alpha')$-structure} has extents
$\lambda_0,\dots,\lambda_{N-1},\alpha,\alpha'_{N+1},\alpha'_{N+2},\dots$:
the lower universes are given, $U_N$ is \emph{cut at} $\alpha$, the higher
universes are arbitrary.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[decoding relative to an oracle]\label{def:El}
An \emph{oracle} is a set $\X$. For a structure and an oracle, define
$\El^\X(c)$ by recursion on the rank of the code $c$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\El^\X(\bot):=\emptyset$, $\El^\X(\top):=\{\star\}$,
  $\El^\X(\code\Prop):=\{\bot,\top\}$, $\El^\X(\code{\mathbf n}):=n$,
  $\El^\X(\code\N):=\omega$;
\item $\El^\X(\code{\U_i})$ is the set of codes in $U_i$ that are
  hereditarily in $\X$: the family of a type is a function from its
  domain to a universe and is admitted like any other function;
\item $\El^\X(\code\Pi(c,G))$ is the set of functions $f\in\X$ with
  $\dom f=\El^\X(c)$ and $f(a)\in\El^\X(G(a))$ for all $a$, if
  $\dom G\supseteq\El^\X(c)$, and $\emptyset$ otherwise;
\item $\El^\X(\code\Sigma(c,G))$ is the set of pairs $\langle a,b\rangle$
  with $a\in\El^\X(c)$ and $b\in\El^\X(G(a))$;
\item $\El^\X(\code W(c,G))$ is the set of well-founded trees, represented
  as sets of finite paths, whose nodes carry labels $a\in\El^\X(c)$ and
  have children indexed by $\El^\X(G(a))$, and such that at every node the
  function sending a child index to the subtree at that child is in $\X$;
\item $\El^\X(\code/(c,r))$ is the set of classes of $\El^\X(c)$ under the
  equivalence relation generated by the pairs $(a,a')$ with
  $r(a)(a')=\top$.
\end{itemize}
By induction on codes $\El^\X(c)\subseteq V_{\rk(c)\cdot\omega}$; this is
why trees are sets of paths and not nested pairs.
\end{definition}

So a function of the decoding has \emph{exactly} the decoding of its
domain as its domain. Two consequences will matter. Functions are
extensional, so equality in the model can be equality of sets, and no
partial equivalence relation is needed. And the oracle is the only
unknown: once $\X$ is given, truth is computed.

\begin{definition}[evaluation]\label{def:eval}
Fix a structure and an oracle $\X$. Terms are \emph{sorted}: each type
former $\Pi$, $\Sigma$, $W$, $/$ that is not a proposition carries the
level $i$ of the sort $\Type_i$ at which it is formed, written
$\Pi^ix{:}A.B$ when it matters, and each subterm that is a proof, that is
whose type is a proposition, carries a mark; a derivation determines
the sorting and the marks, and typing is decidable, so this is
bookkeeping. For a sorted term $t$ and an environment $\rho$ assigning sets to variables
define $\sem t^\X\rho$ by recursion on $t$, returning $\emptyset$, which
is the code $\bot$, wherever a clause does not apply.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\sem x^\X\rho:=\rho(x)$; $\sem\Prop^\X\rho:=\code\Prop$,
  $\sem{\Type_i}^\X\rho:=\code{\U_i}$, $\sem{\mathbf n}^\X\rho:=\code{\mathbf n}$,
  $\sem\N^\X\rho:=\code\N$.
\item \emph{Type formers.} With $c:=\sem A^\X\rho$, the \emph{family at
  level $i$} of the binder $x{:}A$ with body $B$ is the function $G$ on
  $\El^\X(c)$ with $G(a):=\sem B^\X\rho[x{:=}a]$ if this is a code in $U_i$,
  and $G(a):=\bot$ otherwise. Then
  $\sem{\Pi^ix{:}A.B}^\X\rho:=\code\Pi(c,G)$, and likewise $\Sigma^i$ and
  $W^i$; and $\sem{A/R}^\X\rho:=\code/(c,\sem R^\X\rho)$. Families are
  \emph{sanitized}: their values lie in $U_i$ by fiat.
\item \emph{Propositions} are evaluated to truth values, computed from
  the decoding:
  $\sem{\Pi x{:}A.P}^\X\rho:=\top$ iff $\sem P^\X\rho[x{:=}a]=\top$ for
  all $a\in\El^\X(c)$, when $P$ is a proposition;
  $\sem{\|A\|}^\X\rho:=\top$ iff $\El^\X(c)\ne\emptyset$;
  $\sem{a=_Aa'}^\X\rho:=\top$ iff $\sem a^\X\rho=\sem{a'}^\X\rho$;
  $\sem{\Acc_R}^\X\rho$ is the function on $\El^\X(c)$, for $c$ the code of
  the carrier, that is $\top$ exactly on the accessible part of the
  relation $y\prec x:\iff\sem R^\X\rho(y)(x)=\top$ on $\El^\X(c)$.
  Every marked subterm, that is every proof, evaluates to $\star$; this
  covers $\refl$, $|{\cdot}|$, $\mathsf{acc}$, $\mathsf{snd}$,
  $\mathsf{qind}$, $\mathsf{propext}$, $\mathsf{em}$, and abstractions and
  applications of proofs.
\item $\sem{\lambda x{:}A.b}^\X\rho:=$ the function
  $a\mapsto\sem b^\X\rho[x{:=}a]$ on $\El^\X(c)$. It need not belong to
  $\X$. $\sem{t\,u}^\X\rho:=\sem t^\X\rho(\sem u^\X\rho)$ if the first is a
  function with the second in its domain, and $\emptyset$ otherwise.
\item Pairs and projections componentwise; $\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d$ is the
  tree with root label $\sem a^\X\rho$ and subtrees $\sem d^\X\rho(b)$;
  $[a]_R$ is the class of $\sem a^\X\rho$; numerals are numbers.
\item $\ind^{\mathbf n}$ evaluates by cases on the actual argument,
  $\ind^\N$ by primitive recursion, $\ind^W$ by recursion on the
  well-founded tree, all as functions on the decoding of the argument
  type. $\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h$ is the function on the classes that sends
  $q$ to the common value of $\sem f^\X\rho$ on the members of $q$ if
  they agree, and to $\emptyset$ otherwise. $\ind^{\|\|}$ is constantly $\star$.
\item $\sem{\ind^=_Cd}^\X\rho$, where the motive is over $x{:}A$ and
  $e{:}a=_Ax$, is the function sending $x\in\El^\X(c)$ to the function
  that is $\star\mapsto\sem d^\X\rho$ if $x=\sem a^\X\rho$ and the empty
  function otherwise. So \emph{the eliminator of equality checks the
  equation}.
\item $\sem{\ind^{\Acc}_Cf}^\X\rho$ is defined by recursion along $\prec$
  on the accessible part $D$: at $x\in D$ it is
  $\star\mapsto\sem f^\X\rho(x)(\mathrm{rec}_x)$, where $\mathrm{rec}_x$ is
  the function on $\El^\X(c)$ with $\mathrm{rec}_x(y):=\{(\star,v_y)\}$, $v_y$
  the value at $y$, if $y\prec x$, and $\mathrm{rec}_x(y):=\emptyset$, the
  empty function, otherwise; at $x\notin D$ it is the empty function.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[what the evaluation does and does not assert]\label{rem:asserts}
The evaluation asserts nothing: not that a type lands in its universe,
nor that a term lands in the decoding of its type. For the oracle
$\X=V_\theta$ with $\theta$ large it is Werner's evaluation with
universes of arbitrary extent, and its failure to be a model is the
familiar one: $\sem{\Sigma x{:}X.\,f\,x}\rho$ is a code of rank
$\epsilon_0$ when $\rho(f)$ is cofinal, and with sanitized families a
pair $\langle a,b\rangle$ may fail to be in the decoding of its type
because the fibre was replaced by $\bot$. For a general oracle there is
a second failure: the value of an abstraction need not be in $\X$, and
then it is not an element of the function space and everything
downstream is junk. Soundness is a property of particular oracles, and
Section~\ref{sec:model} exhibits one.
\end{remark}

\section{Content, generated environments, closed ordinals}\label{sec:closed}

Throughout this section a level $N$ and ordinals
$\lambda_0<\dots<\lambda_{N-1}$ are fixed, the extents already
determined, and everything is relative to an
$(N,\alpha,\vec\alpha')$-structure and an oracle.

\begin{definition}[content]\label{def:content}
For a code $c$ and $e\in\El^\X(c)$ define the \emph{$N$-content}
$\|e\|_c$, an ordinal, by recursion on $c$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\|e\|_c:=0$ for $c$ one of $\bot,\top,\code\Prop,\code{\mathbf n},\code\N$;
\item for $c=\code{\U_j}$ with any $j$, where $e$ is a code in $U_j$:
  $\|e\|_c:=\rk(e)+1$ if $e\in U_N$, that is if $\rk(e)$ is below the
  cut, and $\|e\|_c:=0$ otherwise;
\item $\|\langle a,b\rangle\|_{\code\Sigma(c',G)}:=\max(\|a\|_{c'},\|b\|_{G(a)})$;
\item $\|t\|_{\code W(c',G)}:=$ the supremum of $\|a\|_{c'}$ over the labels
  $a$ of $t$;
\item $\|q\|_{\code/(c',R)}:=\min\{\|a\|_{c'}:a\in q\}$;
\item $\|f\|_{\code\Pi(c',G)}:=\sup\{\|f(a)\|_{G(a)}:a\in\El^\X(c')\}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The content of an element is the supremum of the ranks of the types of
level at most $N$ that occur in it as values, through all arguments and
components, fibre by fibre. The level of a code is read off the code and
not off the universe it is presented in, since $U_N\subseteq U_{N+1}$: a
family $\N\to\Type_{N+1}$ whose values lie in $U_N$ has the content of
those values. The identity on $U_N$ has content $\alpha$, the cut; so
does a family $\N\to U_N$ of cofinal ranks. If $c\in U_j$ with $j\le N$
then $\|e\|_c\le\rk(c)$ for every $e\in\El^\X(c)$, since a universe
constant mentioned by $c$ has extent below $\rk(c)$.

\begin{definition}[generated environments]\label{def:gen}
A \emph{generation tree} $\theta$ for a context $\Gamma$ assigns to each
variable $x$ of $\Gamma$ either the mark \emph{free} or a triple
$(\Gamma_x,t_x,\theta_x)$ of a context, a term typable in it, and a
generation tree for it; $\theta$ is required to be well founded, hence is
a finite tree, and there are countably many triples $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$
with $t$ typable in $\Gamma$. For an ordinal $\gamma$ and an oracle $\X$, a
\emph{$\gamma$-generated $\X$-environment for $(\Gamma,\theta)$} is an
environment $\rho$ such that, by recursion on $\theta$,
\begin{itemize}
\item $\rho$ is an \emph{$\X$-environment} for $\Gamma$:
  $\rho(x)\in\El^\X(\sem A^\X\rho)$ for every $x{:}A$ in $\Gamma$;
\item if $x$ is free then $\|\rho(x)\|_{\sem A^\X\rho}<\gamma$;
\item if $x$ has the triple $(\Gamma_x,t_x,\theta_x)$ then
  $\rho(x)=\sem{t_x}^{\X_x}\rho_x$ for some oracle $\X_x$ and some
  $\gamma$-generated $\X_x$-environment $\rho_x$ for $(\Gamma_x,\theta_x)$.
\end{itemize}
Each node may use its own oracle. Oracles may be taken to be subsets of
$V_\vartheta$ for $\vartheta$ the supremum of the extents, so for a fixed
structure the $\gamma$-generated environments form a set.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[closed ordinals]\label{def:closedord}
An ordinal $\lambda>\lambda_{N-1}$ (or $\lambda>\omega$ if $N=0$) is
\emph{$N$-closed} if $\beta\cdot\omega<\lambda$ for every $\beta<\lambda$,
and for every triple $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$ and every $\gamma<\lambda$ there
is $\delta<\lambda$ such that: for every sequence $\vec\alpha'$ of higher
extents, every oracle $\X$, and every $\gamma$-generated $\X$-environment
$\rho$ for $(\Gamma,\theta)$ in the $(N,\lambda,\vec\alpha')$-structure,
\[
\text{if }\sem t^\X\rho\text{ is a code of rank below }\lambda
\text{ then its rank is below }\delta.
\]
The extent $\lambda_N$ is the least $N$-closed ordinal, if there is one.
\end{definition}

Closedness is a \emph{gap condition}: a term whose parameters have
content below $\gamma$ cannot take, as the parameters, the oracle, and the
higher extents vary, values in $U_N$ of rank cofinal in $\lambda$. It is
stated in the structure cut at $\lambda$ itself and refers to no higher
extent. It bounds only the values that lie in $U_N$; whether the value
of a term of type $\Type_N$ does lie in $U_N$ is the business of the
soundness induction. For instance, for a definable operator
$t:\Type_N\to\Type_N$ the term $\ind^\N\,\N\,(\lambda\_.\,t)\,x$ with $x{:}\N$
free takes the values $t^n(\N)$; if these are all in $U_N$ they are
bounded below $\lambda$, and then $\Sigma n{:}\N.\,t^n(\N)$ is in $U_N$.
So an $N$-closed ordinal is not reached by iterating a definable type
operator, however fast that operator raises ranks
(Example~\ref{ex:sat}).

\begin{conjecture}[rank lemma]\label{conj:rank}
For every $N$, every triple $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$, and every ordinal
$\gamma$ there is an ordinal $G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)$ such that, for every cut
$\alpha>\max(\gamma,\lambda_{N-1})$, every $\vec\alpha'$, every oracle $\X$,
and every $\gamma$-generated $\X$-environment $\rho$ for $(\Gamma,\theta)$ in
the $(N,\alpha,\vec\alpha')$-structure: if $\sem t^\X\rho$ is a code of
rank below $\alpha$ then its rank is below $G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{lemma}[existence of closed ordinals]\label{lem:closedex}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank} at level $N$. Then an $N$-closed
ordinal exists, so $\lambda_N$ is defined.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)$ least, so that it is monotone in $\gamma$, and
enumerate the triples as $(\Gamma_k,t_k,\theta_k)$. Put
$\alpha_0:=\lambda_{N-1}+1$ (or $\omega+1$) and
$\alpha_{n+1}:=\max(\{\alpha_n\cdot\omega\}\cup\{G_{t_k,\theta_k}(\alpha_n):k\le n\})+1$,
and let $\lambda:=\sup_n\alpha_n$; then $\beta\cdot\omega<\lambda$ for
$\beta<\lambda$. Given the $k$-th triple and $\gamma<\lambda$, choose
$n\ge k$ with $\gamma\le\alpha_n$ and put
$\delta:=G_{t_k,\theta_k}(\alpha_n)<\alpha_{n+1}<\lambda$. The structure
cut at $\lambda$ is one of those the conjecture quantifies over, so in
it every value of $t_k$ at a $\gamma$-generated environment that is a
code of rank below $\lambda$ has rank below
$G_{t_k,\theta_k}(\gamma)\le\delta$.
\end{proof}

\section{The model}\label{sec:model}

Assume that $\lambda_N$ exists for every $N$, and work in the \emph{true
structure}, whose extents are the $\lambda_N$. For each $N$ it is the
$(N,\lambda_N,\vec\lambda_{>N})$-structure. Let
$\lambda_\omega:=\sup_N\lambda_N$; oracles are subsets of
$V_{\lambda_\omega}$.

\begin{definition}[generated elements]\label{def:A}
For each $N$ let $\A_N$ be the set of all values $\sem t^\X\rho$ in
$V_{\lambda_\omega}$, for a triple $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$, an oracle $\X$, and
a $\gamma$-generated $\X$-environment $\rho$ for $(\Gamma,\theta)$ with
$\gamma<\lambda_N$, the content being the $N$-content. The set of
\emph{generated elements} is $\A:=\bigcap_N\A_N$. The \emph{model} is the
evaluation relative to the oracle $\A$: its decoding is $\El:=\El^{\A}$,
its interpretation is $\sem t\rho:=\sem t^{\A}\rho$, and its environments
for $\Gamma$ are the $\A$-environments.
\end{definition}

The definition is not circular: $\A$ is a union over \emph{all} oracles,
and the oracle $\A$ is one of them. Truth in the model is whatever the
evaluation relative to $\A$ computes; it was never needed in order to
define $\A$.

\begin{lemma}[generated elements]\label{lem:gen}
(1) If $e\in\El^\X(c)$ for some oracle $\X$ and some $c\in\El^\X(\code{\U_i})$,
and $\|e\|_c<\lambda_N$, then $e\in\A_N$. (2) If $\rho$ is an
$\X$-environment for $\Gamma$ with all values in $\A_N$ and $t$ is typable
in $\Gamma$, then $\sem t^\X\rho\in\A_N$. (3) If $c\in\El^\X(\code{\U_i})$
then $c\in\A_N$ for every $N$, and every $e\in\El^\X(c)$ is in $\A_N$ for
every $N\ge i$. (4) If $c\in\El(\code{\U_i})$, a type of the model, then
$\El(c)\subseteq\A$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Take the context $X{:}\Type_i,\ x{:}X$ with both variables free,
$\rho(X):=c$, $\rho(x):=e$, and $t:=x$. The content of $c$ as an element
of $\code{\U_i}$ is $\rk(c)+1$ if $c\in U_N$ and $0$ otherwise, in either
case below $\lambda_N$. (2) Graft the generation trees of the values onto
the variables of $\Gamma$; there are finitely many, so the maximum of
their bounds is below $\lambda_N$; and $\rho$ is an $\X$-environment by
hypothesis. (3) The first part was shown in (1); for the second,
$\|e\|_c\le\rk(c)<\lambda_i\le\lambda_N$. (4) By induction on $c$. A
function in $\El(\code\Pi(c,G))$ is in $\A$ by the definition of the
decoding, and codes, numbers, truth values and $\star$ are covered by (1)
and (3). A pair $\langle a,b\rangle$ with $a\in\El(c')$ and
$b\in\El(G(a))$ is the value of $\langle x,y\rangle$ at the type
$\Sigma\_{:}X_1.X_2$ in the context $X_1,X_2{:}\Type_i,\ x{:}X_1,\ y{:}X_2$,
with $\rho(X_1):=c'$ and $\rho(X_2):=G(a)$; this environment is an
$\A$-environment whose values are generated, by induction and (3), so
(2) applies. For a tree use $\mathsf{sup}\,x\,d$ in the context
$X{:}\Type_i,\ Y{:}X\to\Type_i,\ x{:}X,\ d{:}Y\,x\to Wz{:}X.\,Y\,z$ with
$\rho(Y):=G$: since $c$ is hereditarily in $\A$, the family $G$ is in
$\A$ and is an element of $\El(\sem{X\to\Type_i}\rho)$; the root label is
generated by induction and the subtree function is in $\A$ by the
definition of the decoding. For a class use $[x]_R$ with $\rho(R):=r$,
the relation of the code, which is in $\A$ for the same reason.
\end{proof}

The role of the clause for universes in Definition~\ref{def:El} is
visible in (4): a tree of a $W$-code whose family is not generated need
not be generated, and Lemma~\ref{lem:tyform} needs every value of an
environment to be. Such a code would be inert in any case, since in an
intensional theory a variable type cannot be eliminated; but it is
simpler not to admit it.

Only the types that are not small at level $N$ are restricted by
$\A_N$: a function $\Type_N\to\Type_N$ must be the value of a term whose
parameters have content below $\lambda_N$, while every element of a type
in $U_N$ is its own parameter at level $N$ and above, by (3). Membership
in $\A$ asks for this at every level, and the lower levels do restrict
the small types of level $N$: a sequence $\N\to U_{N-1}$ of cofinal ranks
is excluded from $\N\to\Type_N$ as it is from $\N\to\Type_{N-1}$, which is
harmless and is what cumulativity wants.


\begin{lemma}[type formation]\label{lem:tyform}
Let $\rho$ be an environment of the model for $\Gamma$, suppose
$\Gamma,x{:}A\vdash B:\Type_N$ is derivable, and suppose
$c:=\sem A\rho\in U_N$. Then $\sem{\Pi^Nx{:}A.B}\rho$,
$\sem{\Sigma^Nx{:}A.B}\rho$, and $\sem{W^Nx{:}A.B}\rho$ lie in $U_N$;
likewise for quotients.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The values of $\rho$ lie in $\A\subseteq\A_N$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:gen}(4),
so each is $\sem{t_y}^{\X_y}\rho_y$ for a $\gamma_y$-generated environment
with $\gamma_y<\lambda_N$. Let $\theta$ be the generation tree for
$\Gamma,x{:}A$ that gives each $y$ of $\Gamma$ its triple and marks $x$
free, and let $\gamma:=\max(\{\gamma_y\}\cup\{\rk(c)+1\})<\lambda_N$. For
every $a\in\El(c)$ we have $\|a\|_c\le\rk(c)<\gamma$, so $\rho[x{:=}a]$ is a
$\gamma$-generated $\A$-environment for $(\Gamma,x{:}A;\theta)$ in the true
structure. The family $G$ at level $N$ takes at $a$ the value
$\sem B\rho[x{:=}a]$ if this is a code of rank below $\lambda_N$, and
$\bot$ otherwise. Since $\lambda_N$ is $N$-closed there is
$\delta<\lambda_N$, depending only on $(B,\theta,\gamma)$, that bounds the
rank of every such value; so every value of $G$ has rank below $\delta$.
The code $\code Q(c,G)$ is a pair of $c$ and $G$, a function on
$\El(c)\subseteq V_{\rk(c)\cdot\omega}$; so its rank is below
$\max(\rk(c)\cdot\omega,\delta)+\omega<\lambda_N$.
\end{proof}

The division of labour is this. That an \emph{individual} fibre
$\sem B\rho[x{:=}a]$ lies in $U_N$ is the induction hypothesis of the
soundness proof for the premise $\Gamma,x{:}A\vdash B:\Type_N$; so in the
model the sanitization changes nothing. That the fibres are
\emph{uniformly} bounded, so that the code of the family lies in $U_N$,
is closedness.

\begin{lemma}[soundness]\label{lem:sound}
Let $\rho$ be an environment of the model for $\Gamma$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Gamma\vdash A:\Type_i$ then $\sem A\rho\in U_i$; if
  $\Gamma\vdash P:\Prop$ then $\sem P\rho$ is a truth value.
\item If $\Gamma\vdash t:A$ then $\sem t\rho\in\El(\sem A\rho)$.
\item If $\Gamma\vdash t\equiv u:A$ then $\sem t\rho=\sem u\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By simultaneous induction on derivations, with the usual substitution
lemma $\sem{t[u/x]}\rho=\sem t\rho[x{:=}\sem u\rho]$. This is Werner's
argument; we go through the cases that differ.
\emph{Abstraction.} $\sem{\lambda x{:}A.b}\rho$ is a function on
$\El(\sem A\rho)$ with values in the right fibres by the induction
hypothesis for $b$ at the environments $\rho[x{:=}a]$, which are
environments of the model; and it is in $\A$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:gen}(2),(4). So it is in the decoding of the product.
\emph{Application.} The argument lies in the decoding of the domain,
which is exactly the domain of the function.
\emph{Type formers} at sort $\Type_i$: at every $a\in\El(\sem A\rho)$ the
fibre $\sem B\rho[x{:=}a]$ lies in $U_i$ by the induction hypothesis for
$B$, so it is the value of the sanitized family there, and the code
lies in $U_i$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:tyform} at level $i$, the premises
$A:\Type_i$ and $B:\Type_i$ being available by cumulativity. It is
hereditarily in $\A$: its family is the value of $\lambda x{:}A.B$, which
is generated, its domain and fibres are types of the model by the
induction hypothesis, and for a quotient the relation is the value of
$R$. So the code is in $\El(\code{\U_i})$. Propositions
are truth values, in $U_0$, so the impredicative product needs no bound.
\emph{Trees.} The subtree function of $\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d$ at the root is
$\sem d\rho$, which is in $\A$, and at deeper nodes it is the subtree
function of a subtree, which is an element of the model.
\emph{Computation rules} hold as equations between sets: $\beta$ by the
substitution lemma; $\eta$ because a function of the decoding has
exactly the decoding of its domain as its domain; the rules for
$\ind^{\mathbf n}$, $\ind^\N$, $\ind^W$ by the recursions that define
them; $\ind^=_Cd\,a\,(\refl\,a)\equiv d$ because the check succeeds. For
$\ind^{\Acc}$ the right-hand side applies $\sem f\rho(x)$ to the value of
$\lambda y\,r.\,\ind^{\Acc}_Cf\,y\,(h\,y\,r)$, which is the function
$\mathrm{rec}_x$ of Definition~\ref{def:eval} on the nose, the inner
abstraction having the domain $\El$ of the truth value of $R\,y\,x$; and
$\mathrm{rec}_x$ is in $\A$ because it is the value of that term. For
$\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h\,[a]_R\equiv f\,a$: the premise $h$ is a proof, so
its type is true, so $\sem f\rho$ takes equal values, as sets, on
$R$-related elements and hence on the members of a class.
\emph{Elimination of equality.} If $e:a=_Ax$ in the context of the rule
then the truth value of $a=_Ax$ is $\top$, that is
$\sem a\rho=\sem x\rho$; so the check succeeds and
$C[a,\refl]$ and $C[x,e]$ have the same code, proofs being all $\star$.
\emph{Proof irrelevance, $\mathsf{propext}$, $\mathsf{em}$.} Every proof is
$\star$; two equivalent propositions have the same truth value, which
is equality of codes; and $\El(\code\Prop)$ has two elements.
\emph{Cumulativity.} $U_i\subseteq U_{i+1}$, truth values are in $U_0$,
and $\code{\U_i}\in U_{i+1}$ because $\lambda_i\cdot\omega<\lambda_{i+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank}. Then the evaluation relative to $\A$ is
a model of $\CIC_\omega+\EM$ in which $\bot$ is empty. So if $\ZF$ proves
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank} then $\ZF\vdash\Con(\CIC_\omega+\EM)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:closedex} and \ref{lem:sound}; $\sem\bot\rho$ is the
truth value of $\|\mathbf 0\|$, which is $\bot$, and $\El(\bot)=\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:sound} is written at the level of its cases and has not
been checked against every rule of Section~\ref{sec:tt}. Three points
deserve a second look when it is: that the sorting and the proof marks
which the evaluation reads are determined by the derivation and are
stable under conversion; that the accessible part computed in the
decoding agrees with the inductive reading of $\Acc$ that the
introduction rule needs; and the substitution lemma under sanitized
families.

\section{The rank lemma}\label{sec:ranklemma}

Everything rests on Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank}. We say why we believe
it, what a proof has to contain, and what is known to obstruct the
obvious proof.

\paragraph{The first-order case.}
For a term all of whose variables are free and of small type the
conjecture is the rank lemma of \cite[\S3]{paper2} in type-theoretic
dress. A type former adds a bounded amount to the ranks of its
components. $\ind^\N$ with a type-valued motive iterates the bound of its
step, and the supremum over $n<\omega$ of the iterates is an ordinal. A
$W$-recursion over a tree with small branching iterates the bound of its
step along the height of the tree, which is below the Hartogs number of
its branching types. An $\Acc$-recursion needs an idea: the accessible
part of a relation on a large carrier has unbounded height, but the
value at $x$ depends on the recursive function only at the predecessors
at which the step applies it, which are the values of finitely many
subterms in environments varying in bound variables of small type; the
tree of inspected predecessors has small branching, so its height is
below a Hartogs number, as for well-founded recursion in \cite{paper2}.
None of these bounds mentions the cut.

\paragraph{Higher types.}
A term may bind a variable of a type that is not small, such as
$G:\Type_N\to\Type_N$, and apply a defined functional to the identity,
whose content is the cut. So the bound for a term of function type must
be a function of the bounds of its arguments, hereditarily: a
\emph{profile}, a majorant in Howard's sense \cite{howard73} with
ordinals for numbers. The statement to prove is a fundamental lemma:
the value of a term has the profile computed from the profiles of its
variables by recursion on the term, and the profiles so computed do not
depend on the cut. Since terms are now typed, and every value lives in
the decoding of a code, profiles can be assigned by recursion on the
code, which is well founded; no universal domain of profiles is needed.
Three things are known to be delicate.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Bounds are monotone but not continuous}, because the rank of
  $\Sigma n{:}\N.\,B(n)$ is a genuine supremum. So a recursion cannot be
  bounded by a least fixed point: the step
  $\mathrm{rec}\mapsto(\Sigma n{:}\N.\,\mathrm{rec}\,(p\,x\,n))\to\Prop$ has the
  bound $o\mapsto o+k$, whose only fixed point is ``no bound'', while the
  truth is a countable ordinal. The inspection argument is essential.
\item \emph{The inspected predecessors are not simply those at which the
  recursive function is applied.} Let $K$ be a parameter of bounded
  content. A step of the form
  $K(\lambda X{:}\Type_N.\,\mathrm{rec}\,(h\,X))$ evaluates the recursive
  function at $h(X)$ for every $X$ in the universe.
  What is small is the set of predecessors whose values can reach the
  \emph{rank} of the result, the value of $K$ being bounded whatever it
  is given. So the profile of the recursive function must depend on the
  actual predecessor and not only on a bound for it.
\item \emph{Large elimination makes the shape of a value depend on
  data.} For $B:\N\to\Type_{N+1}$ with $B\,0=\Type_N$ and
  $B\,(n{+}1)=B\,n\to B\,n$, an element of $B\,n$ is a functional of order
  $n$ over the universe. Profiles indexed by codes handle this, but they
  must be joined fibrewise and never across the branches of an
  eliminator whose branches have different levels
  (Example~\ref{ex:hetero}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Anatomy of a proof.}
We record the architecture we expect a proof to have, as a plan and not
a result. \emph{Choice is free}: $\Con(\CIC_\omega+\EM)$ is an arithmetic
statement, hence absolute to $L$, so the whole construction may be
carried out in $L$ and may use the axiom of choice and the generalized
continuum hypothesis. \emph{The relation.} In a fixed structure and
oracle, define $e\lhd p$, ``the profile $p$ bounds $e$'', by recursion on
the membership relation on $e$, not on a code: a code of rank below the
cut must have rank below the ordinal part of $p$; a pair is bounded
componentwise; a function $e$ is bounded if $a\lhd q$ implies
$e(a)\lhd p\langle q\rangle$, where $p\langle q\rangle$ applies the function
part of $p$ by monotone extension; a class is bounded if some member
is; numbers, proofs, truth values, and the junk value $\emptyset$ are
bounded by everything. The relation depends on the cut, but profiles do
not mention it. \emph{Constants.} Add profiles $\hat\gamma$ with
$e\lhd\hat\gamma$ iff every value of $e$, hereditarily, is so bounded; a
parameter of content below $\gamma$ satisfies this, and so does every
element of a type of rank below $\gamma$. This is what removes large
suprema from the calculus: the family of a type former over a small
domain is bounded by evaluating the profile of its body once, at the
constant of the domain, and not by a supremum over the elements of the
domain. The suprema that remain are over $\N$, for $\ind^\N$, and along
ordinals of Hartogs length, for $W$ and $\Acc$; both exist by
Replacement, because the functions being iterated are definable class
functions on all the ordinals and not functions below a fixed bound.
This is the point at which the argument escapes the cofinality
obstruction of Example~\ref{ex:climb}: the iteration is performed in the
universe, as in \cite[\S3]{paper2}, and $\lambda_N$ is a closure point of
the result. \emph{The fundamental lemma} then says that the value of a
term is bounded by the profile computed from the profiles of its
variables by recursion on the term, in every structure and under every
oracle; the clauses for application, abstraction, pairs, type formers,
$\ind^{\mathbf n}$, $\ind^\N$, $\ind^=$, and $\mathsf{lift}$ go through as
for Howard's theorem, junk and sanitized fibres included, because both
are bounded by everything.

Two things are missing. The first is a domain for the profiles of
abstractions. A profile is a set, so the function part of the profile of
$\lambda x{:}A.b$ must be defined on a set of argument profiles, and
soundness needs every argument the function can receive to have a
profile in that set. Stratifying by the order of the type of $x$ works
for simple types; here a bound type variable can be instantiated, by a
term, at a type of any order, and large elimination produces types whose
order depends on a number, so the order of an argument is not read off
the type of $x$. We expect a stratification by ordinals assigned to
subterms, solvable because the universes are predicative, but have not
found the right invariant. The second is the inspection argument for $W$
and $\Acc$, described above, which needs the profile of the recursive
function to depend on the actual predecessor.


The cut enters the evaluation in four ways. It is the range of a bound
variable of universe type; but a type former whose domain is not in
$U_N$ has a code of rank at least the cut, which the conjecture does not
constrain, while one whose domain is in $U_N$ binds a variable whose
values have content bounded by the rank of the domain; and an
abstraction over a larger type is only ever applied to arguments that
have themselves been bounded. It is the threshold at which families are
sanitized; but a fibre replaced by $\bot$ only lowers the rank. It
decides when an application is junk, because the argument fell outside
the universe; but junk is $\emptyset$. And it changes the truth of
propositions, through the oracle and through the extents; but the
conjecture quantifies over all oracles and extents, and a proposition
can influence a type only through the relation of a quotient, the
domain of an $\Acc$-recursion, the check in $\ind^=$, and the decoding of
a proof type, each of which selects among alternatives that are bounded
anyway.

\paragraph{Attacks that failed.}
In the extensional setting of \cite[\S8]{paper2}, where the conjecture
was stated for untyped terms and arbitrary truth assignments and is
therefore stronger, the following attempts to produce types just below
the cut from parameters of bounded content failed: ill-typed terms
binding a universe; parameters of content $0$ over a large carrier
without codes, such as a well-order of length above the cut on
$\Type_N\to\Prop$ with a countable predecessor function, driving an
$\Acc$-recursion of a Hartogs-raising step (the inspected tree is
countable, and after an overflow the junk value restarts the iteration
from the bottom); trees of large height and content $0$ with large
branching; classes of a quotient of the universe containing both a
small code and a code near the cut; and selection of a code by a
proposition, which would need a description operator. The present
formulation has not yet been attacked on its own terms, and the oracle
is a new degree of freedom for an adversary: an oracle may contain a
cofinal family, and the conjecture claims that a term with bounded
parameters cannot get at it.

\section{A proved case: two universes, recursion on $\N$ only}\label{sec:proved}

We prove the rank lemma for $\CIC_2^-$, the theory of
Section~\ref{sec:tt} with the sorts $\Prop:\Type_0:\Type_1$ only, $\Type_1$
not being a type, and without $W$-types and $\Acc$. It keeps $\Pi$,
$\Sigma$, finite types, $\N$ with elimination into every sort, identity,
truncation, quotients, $\mathsf{propext}$ and $\mathsf{em}$. Two features
make this case tractable. In $\CIC_2$ nothing quantifies over $\Type_1$,
so the types that are not small have a \emph{simple kind} fixed by their
syntax, and profiles live in the finite-type hierarchy over the
ordinals, as in Howard's theorem. And without $W$ and $\Acc$ the only
supremum is over $\N$, so all bounds stay below a regular cardinal.
Neither holds in general; what this case shows is that the architecture
of Section~\ref{sec:ranklemma} closes when they do.

We work in $\ZFC$, which is harmless by the remark on $L$ in
Section~\ref{sec:ranklemma}. The level is $N=0$, a structure is a single
cut $\alpha>\omega$, and there are no higher extents.

\subsection{Kinds}

A type $A$ in a context is \emph{small} if $A:\Type_0$ or $A:\Prop$ is
derivable, and \emph{big} otherwise.

\begin{lemma}[big types are rigid]\label{lem:rigid}
In $\CIC_2^-$ a big type is, as a raw term, $\Type_0$ or one of
$\Pi x{:}A.B$, $\Sigma x{:}A.B$, $A/R$ with a big component. Bigness is
invariant under substitution and conversion, and two convertible big
types have the same head former and convertible components.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A big type that is not a former and not $\Type_0$ would be a variable,
an application, a projection, or an eliminator term $u$ with
$\Gamma\vdash u:\Type_1$. Each of these receives its type from a type
that is itself a term: the type of a variable from the context, the
type of an application from the codomain of a product, the type of an
eliminator from its motive. But $\Type_1$ is not a term, so it is not
the type of a variable, a codomain, or a motive. Computation acts inside
the components of a former and never at its head, which gives the rest,
given the standard metatheory of the intensional theory (confluence and
subject reduction), which we assume.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[kinds]\label{def:kinds}
\emph{Kinds} are $\kappa::=o\mid\kappa\to\kappa\mid\kappa\times\kappa$. The kind
$k(A)$ of a type is $o$ if $A$ is small or $A=\Type_0$; otherwise
$k(\Pi x{:}A.B):=k(A)\to k(B)$, $k(\Sigma x{:}A.B):=k(A)\times k(B)$, and
$k(A/R):=k(A)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} this is well defined,
$k(B[u/x])=k(B)$, and convertible types have the same kind; so a term
has a kind, the kind of its type.
\end{definition}

In this section the code of a big type carries its kind,
$\code\Pi^\kappa(c,G)$ and so on, which the evaluation can supply because
terms are sorted; an oracle is a family $(\X_\kappa)_\kappa$ of sets, the
decoding of a code of kind $\kappa$ admits the functions in $\X_\kappa$, and
a small code has kind $o$. In a generation tree the term $t_x$ of a
defined variable $x$ is required to have the kind of $x$. This is
bookkeeping: it ensures that an element of the model is generated at
the kind at which it is used.

\subsection{Profiles and the bounding relation}

Fix a regular uncountable cardinal $\Theta$.

\begin{definition}[profiles]\label{def:profiles}
$D_o:=\{\beta:1\le\beta\le\Theta\}$, ordered as ordinals, with $\Theta$
as top element, read as ``no bound''; $D_{\kappa\to\kappa'}$ is the set of
monotone functions from $D_\kappa$ to $D_{\kappa'}$, ordered pointwise; and
$D_{\kappa\times\kappa'}:=D_\kappa\times D_{\kappa'}$. Each $D_\kappa$ is a complete
lattice. A profile is \emph{proper} if it is below $\Theta$ at kind $o$,
maps proper profiles to proper profiles at $\kappa\to\kappa'$, and has
proper components at $\kappa\times\kappa'$. For $\gamma<\Theta$ the
\emph{constant} $\hat\gamma_\kappa$ is $\gamma$ at kind $o$, the constant
function with value $\hat\gamma_{\kappa'}$ at $\kappa\to\kappa'$, and the pair
of constants at $\kappa\times\kappa'$; it is proper.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[pure codes, the bounding relation]\label{def:lhd}
A code is \emph{pure} if no universe constant, of any structure, occurs
in it: the constants other than the $\langle\mathsf U,\xi\rangle$ are pure,
$\code Q(c,G)$ is pure if $c$ and all values of $G$ are, and $\code/(c,r)$
is pure if $c$ is. For a set $e$ and $p\in D_\kappa$ define $e\lhd_\kappa p$ by
recursion on $\kappa$ and, at kind $o$, on membership:
\begin{itemize}
\item at every kind, a class is bounded by $p$ iff some member of it is;
\item $e\lhd_o\beta$, for $e$ not a class, iff $e$ is a number or $\star$;
  or $e$ is a pure code whose rank is finite or below $\beta$, the
  condition on the rank being dropped when $\beta=\Theta$, the top
  element; or $e$ is a pair both of whose components are
  $\lhd_o\beta$; or $e$ is a function all of whose values are
  $\lhd_o\beta$;
\item $e\lhd_{\kappa\to\kappa'}p$, for $e$ not a class, iff $e$ is not a code
  of infinite rank and, for every $a\in\dom e$ and every $q\in D_\kappa$,
  if $a\lhd_\kappa q$ then $e(a)\lhd_{\kappa'}p(q)$; the second condition
  holds vacuously if $e$ is not a function;
\item $e\lhd_{\kappa\times\kappa'}(p,p')$, for $e$ not a class, iff $e$ is
  not a code of infinite rank and either $e$ is not a pair or
  $e=\langle a,b\rangle$ with $a\lhd_\kappa p$ and $b\lhd_{\kappa'}p'$.
\end{itemize}
A set is \emph{flat} if it is $\lhd_o\Theta$. We extend the decoding by
$\El^\X(e):=\emptyset$ when $e$ is not a code.
\end{definition}

The relation does not depend on the structure, the oracle, or a code.
In particular a pure code is bounded only by ordinals above its rank,
whether or not that rank is below the cut: the bounds below are bounds
on the values themselves, and the cut enters only through the
sanitized families, which can only lower a rank.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lhdbasic}
(1) If $e\lhd_\kappa p\le p'$ then $e\lhd_\kappa p'$. (2) $\emptyset$ is
bounded by every profile at every kind. (3) If $c$ is a pure code then
every element of $\El^\X(c)$, for any oracle, is $\lhd_o1$. (4) If
$e\lhd_o\beta$ and $e$ is a function then $e(a)\lhd_o\beta$ for every
$a\in\dom e$. (5) Suprema: if $e_n\lhd_\kappa p_n$ for all $n$ then each
$e_n\lhd_\kappa\bigsqcup_np_n$. (6) Proper profiles are closed under
application, pairing, projection, finite joins, and suprema of
countable families.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) By induction on $\kappa$ and on membership. (2) $\emptyset$ is the
pure code $\bot$, of finite rank, and it is neither a pair nor a function
with an argument. (3) By induction on $c$: the elements of a pure code
are numbers, $\star$, truth values, which are pure codes of finite rank,
and pairs, functions, and classes built from elements of pure codes,
the fibres of a pure code being pure. (4) By the clause at $o$. (5) By (1). (6) At kind
$o$ the supremum of countably many ordinals below $\Theta$ is below
$\Theta$ because $\Theta$ is regular and uncountable; at the other kinds
suprema and joins are pointwise and componentwise, so the claim follows
by induction on the kind.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Pure profiles of terms}

\begin{definition}\label{def:P}
For a term $t$ with $\Gamma\vdash t:T$ and an assignment $\pi$ of a
profile $\pi(x)\in D_{k(\Gamma(x))}$ to each variable, define
$P_t(\pi)\in D_{k(T)}$ by recursion on $t$. Write $p\cdot q$ for $p(q)$
when the product type of the function is big, and for $p$ when it is
small (a small product has kind $o$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(4) a bound for a function of kind $o$ bounds
its values).
\begin{itemize}
\item $P_x(\pi):=\pi(x)$. Every proof, every proposition, and each of
  $\Prop$, $\N$, $\mathbf n$, numerals, and $\mathbf k_{\mathbf n}$ has
  profile $1$.
\item $P_{f\,u}(\pi):=P_f(\pi)\cdot P_u(\pi)$.
\item $P_{\lambda x{:}A.b}(\pi)$ is the function
  $q\mapsto P_b(\pi[x{:=}q])$ on $D_{k(A)}$ if the product is big, and
  $P_b(\pi[x{:=}1])$ if it is small.
\item Pairs and projections are componentwise at a big sum; at a small
  sum the profile of a pair is the maximum of the profiles of its
  components and the profile of a projection is that of its argument.
\item For a small type former, with $\beta:=P_A(\pi)$ and
  $\beta':=P_B(\pi[x{:=}1])$, the profile of $\Pi x{:}A.B$ and of
  $\Sigma x{:}A.B$ is $\min(\Theta,\max(\beta\cdot\omega,\beta')+\omega)$,
  and the profile of $A/R$ is $\min(\Theta,\beta\cdot\omega+\omega)$.
\item $P_{[a]_R}(\pi):=P_a(\pi)$, and
  $P_{\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h}(\pi)$ is $q\mapsto P_f(\pi)\cdot q$, or
  $P_f(\pi)$ at a small product.
\item $P_{\ind^{\mathbf n}_C\vec c}(\pi)$ is the constant function with
  value $\bigsqcup_jP_{c_j}(\pi)$, or that value itself at a small
  product. The branches have the same kind, the kind of $C$.
\item $P_{\ind^\N_Cc\,g}(\pi)$ is the constant function with value
  $\bigsqcup_n\mu_n$, or that value, where $\mu_0:=P_c(\pi)$ and
  $\mu_{n+1}:=(P_g(\pi)\cdot1)\cdot\mu_n$.
\item $P_{\ind^=_Cd}(\pi)$ is the function constantly $P_d(\pi)$ in both
  arguments, or $P_d(\pi)$.
\end{itemize}
A big type is not a term of any type and has no profile.
\end{definition}

By induction on $t$, $P_t(\pi)$ is monotone in $\pi$, which is what
makes the abstraction clause land in $D_{k(A)\to k(B)}$.

\begin{lemma}[fundamental lemma]\label{lem:fund}
Let $\Gamma\vdash t:T$ in $\CIC_2^-$. For every cut $\alpha$, every oracle
$\X$, every environment $\rho$, and every $\pi$ with
$\rho(x)\lhd_{k(\Gamma(x))}\pi(x)$ for all $x$:
$\sem t^\X\rho\lhd_{k(T)}P_t(\pi)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By induction on $t$. The environment need not be typed.
\emph{Variables} by hypothesis. \emph{Proofs, propositions, constants:}
the values are $\star$, truth values, or pure codes of finite rank, all
$\lhd_o1$. \emph{Application.} If the product is big, the value is
$F(A)$ with $F\lhd P_f$ and $A\lhd P_u$ by induction, so
$F(A)\lhd P_f(P_u)$ by the clause for functions, or it is $\emptyset$. If
the product is small, the value is a value of $F\lhd_oP_f$ or is
$\emptyset$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(4),(2) apply.
\emph{Abstraction at a big product.} The value is the function
$a\mapsto\sem b^\X\rho[x{:=}a]$; if $a\lhd q$ then
$\rho[x{:=}a]\lhd\pi[x{:=}q]$, so the induction hypothesis for $b$ gives
$\sem b^\X\rho[x{:=}a]\lhd P_b(\pi[x{:=}q])$.
\emph{Abstraction at a small product.} Then $A$ is small, so by
induction $c:=\sem A^\X\rho\lhd_oP_A(\pi)$; so if $c$ is a code it is
pure, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(3) every $a\in\El^\X(c)$ is
$\lhd_o1$, while if $c$ is not a code there is no such $a$. So every value
$\sem b^\X\rho[x{:=}a]$ is $\lhd_oP_b(\pi[x{:=}1])$, and the function is
flat and bounded by it.
\emph{Pairs and projections:} componentwise, a projection of a non-pair
being $\emptyset$.
\emph{Small type formers.} The value is $\code Q(c,G)$ with
$c\lhd_o\beta$, so $c$ is pure; every $a\in\El^\X(c)$ is $\lhd_o1$, so each
fibre, which is $\sem B^\X\rho[x{:=}a]$ or $\bot$, is
$\lhd_o\beta'$ by induction, in particular pure. So the code is pure. The
ranks of $c$ and of the fibres are finite or below $\beta$ and $\beta'$;
the elements of $\El^\X(c)$ have rank below
$\rk(c)\cdot\omega$; so the rank of the code is below
$\max(\beta\cdot\omega,\beta')+\omega$, which is the clause at $o$ for a
pure code. If $c$ is not a code the family is empty and the bound holds
the more.
For a quotient the relation is a function on $\El^\X(c)$ into functions
into truth values, of rank below $\rk(c)\cdot\omega+\omega$.
\emph{Classes:} $\sem{[a]_R}$ has the member $\sem a$. For
$\mathsf{lift}_Cf\,h$ at a class $q\lhd p$, some member $a_0$ has
$a_0\lhd p$, and the value is $F(a_0)$, the common value, or $\emptyset$.
\emph{$\ind^{\mathbf n}$:} the value at $k$ is the value of $c_k$, bounded
by the join by Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(1).
\emph{$\ind^\N$:} by induction on $n$ the value $v_n$ at $n$ is
$\lhd\mu_n$, since $v_{n+1}=\sem g(n)(v_n)$, $n\lhd_o1$, and
$\sem g\lhd P_g$; then Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(5). If the argument is not
a number the value is $\emptyset$.
\emph{$\ind^=$:} the value at $x$ and $\star$ is $\sem d$ or undefined.
\emph{Conversion:} convertible types have the same kind, so the
statement for $t:A$ is the statement for $t:B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[properness]\label{lem:proper}
If every $\pi(x)$ is proper then $P_t(\pi)$ is proper.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By induction on $t$, using Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic}(6) and that
$\Theta$, being an uncountable cardinal, is closed under
$\beta\mapsto\beta\cdot\omega+\omega$. For an abstraction, if $q$ is proper
then so is $\pi[x{:=}q]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The rank lemma and the closed ordinal}

Closedness is as in Definition~\ref{def:closedord} with $N=0$ and no
higher extents, a free variable $x{:}A$ being required to satisfy
$\rho(x)\lhd_{k(A)}\hat\gamma_{k(A)}$. This is implied by $N$-content below
$\gamma$ for an element of the decoding of the code of $A$, by induction
on the structure of a big type, the base cases being a small type, whose
code is pure by Lemma~\ref{lem:fund} applied along the context so that
its elements are $\lhd_o1$, and $\Type_0$, whose elements are pure codes
of rank below $\gamma$. In the step for a product the environment is
extended by an element of the decoding of the domain, which is bounded
by the top profile of its kind, by the same induction with $\Theta$ in
place of $\gamma$; Lemma~\ref{lem:fund} does not need its profiles to be
proper.

\begin{theorem}[rank lemma for $\CIC_2^-$]\label{thm:rankcic2}
$(\ZFC)$ Let $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$ be a triple of $\CIC_2^-$ and $\gamma$ an
ordinal, and put $\Theta:=\max(|\gamma|,\omega)^+$. There is an ordinal
$G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)<\Theta$ such that for every cut $\alpha$, every oracle,
and every $\gamma$-generated environment $\rho$ for $(\Gamma,\theta)$: if
$\sem t^\X\rho$ is a code of infinite rank, then its rank is below
$G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\Theta$ is a successor cardinal, hence regular, and $\gamma<\Theta$. By
recursion on $\theta$ define $\pi_\theta$: a free variable $x{:}A$ gets
$\hat\gamma_{k(A)}$, and a variable with the triple
$(\Gamma_x,t_x,\theta_x)$ gets $P_{t_x}(\pi_{\theta_x})$, a profile of the
kind of $x$ by the requirement on generation trees. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:proper} every $\pi_\theta(x)$ is proper, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:fund}, applied at each node with that node's oracle,
every $\gamma$-generated $\rho$ has $\rho(x)\lhd\pi_\theta(x)$; this uses
that the relation does not depend on the oracle. So
$\sem t^\X\rho\lhd P_t(\pi_\theta)$, which is proper. If $t$ has kind $o$,
put $G_{t,\theta}(\gamma):=P_t(\pi_\theta)<\Theta$; a value that is a code
of infinite rank has rank below it by the clause at $o$. If
$t$ has another kind then its value is not a code of infinite rank, by
the clauses at $\kappa\to\kappa'$ and $\kappa\times\kappa'$, and any ordinal
serves.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:omega1}
$(\ZFC)$ For $\CIC_2^-$, every uncountable cardinal is $0$-closed; in
particular $\lambda_0\le\omega_1$ exists.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $\lambda$ be an uncountable cardinal and $\gamma<\lambda$. Then
$\max(|\gamma|,\omega)^+\le\lambda$, so
$\delta:=G_{t,\theta}(\gamma)<\lambda$ bounds the values of $t$ that are
codes of rank below $\lambda$ in the structure cut at $\lambda$; and
$\beta\cdot\omega<\lambda$ for $\beta<\lambda$.
\end{proof}

So the definable types of $\CIC_2^-$ have countable rank, uniformly in
countable parameters, which is as it should be for a theory whose only
recursion is on $\N$. With Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, specialised to one
cut and kind-indexed oracles, this gives a model of $\CIC_2^-+\EM$ in
$L$, hence $\ZF\vdash\Con(\CIC_2^-+\EM)$, subject to the rule-by-rule check
of Lemma~\ref{lem:sound}. That consistency statement is not new; what
the case establishes is that the definitions of
Sections~\ref{sec:sem}--\ref{sec:model} are satisfiable and that the
rank lemma can be proved by majorization when the two obstacles below
are absent.

\paragraph{What the general case adds.}
\emph{$W$ and $\Acc$} replace the supremum over $\N$ by iterations of
Hartogs length, which no single regular $\Theta$ below an inaccessible
absorbs, since the length depends on a bound that is itself below
$\Theta$; the iteration has to be performed in the universe, by
Replacement, and the properness predicate replaced by a statement about
families coherent in $\Theta$; and the inspection argument of
Section~\ref{sec:ranklemma} is needed to bound the length.
\emph{More universes} bring type variables of sort $\Type_{N+1}$ and
types of that sort computed by recursion, so that the kind of a term is
no longer fixed by its syntax: Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} fails, and the
profile of a polymorphic term is a family indexed by kinds. The
universes being predicative, we expect kinds themselves to be
stratified by level, a kind of level $j$ being a set-sized object built
from the kinds of lower levels; this has not been worked out.

\section{Two universes with $W$-types and accessibility}\label{sec:cic2}

We extend Section~\ref{sec:proved} to $\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$: the theory with
the sorts $\Prop:\Type_0:\Type_1$ in which $Wx{:}A.B$ is formed only when
the branching type $B$ is small, the label type $A$ being arbitrary, and
$\Acc_R$ only when the carrier $X$ is small; the eliminators $\ind^W$ and
$\ind^{\Acc}$ go into every sort. This is the fragment that the
interpretation of $\ZFh$ uses \cite[\S6]{paper2}: there
$\mathsf{set}=Wx{:}\Type_0.\,x$ has big labels and small branching, and
well-founded recursion is $\Acc$-recursion on the index type of a set,
which is small, with motive $\mathsf{set}$. The restriction is what makes
the \emph{full} height of a recursion bounded by the bounds of its data,
so that no inspection argument is needed; without it we have no proof
(see the end of the section). The new difficulty is that the recursions
have Hartogs length, which a regular cardinal below an inaccessible does
not absorb. We replace regularity by elementarity, and no longer need the
axiom of choice.

\subsection{Trees}

Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} extends: a big type is $\Type_0$, or a $\Pi$, a
$\Sigma$, a quotient with a big component, or $Wx{:}A.B$ with $A$ big.
Kinds get a constructor $T(\kappa)$, with $k(Wx{:}A.B):=T(k(A))$ for a big
$W$-type; $D_{T(\kappa)}:=D_\kappa$, a profile of a tree being a bound for all
its labels. The bounding relation gets two clauses: at kind $o$, a tree
is $\lhd_o\beta$ iff all its labels are; and $e\lhd_{T(\kappa)}p$ iff $e$ is
not a code of infinite rank and, if $e$ is a tree, every label of $e$,
at every node, is $\lhd_\kappa p$. Lemma~\ref{lem:lhdbasic} holds as before,
the elements of a pure $W$-code being trees with labels and indices from
pure codes.

Put $\hbar(\beta):=\hartogs(V_{\beta\cdot\omega+\omega+1})$, the least ordinal that
does not inject into that set. It bounds the two heights we need, in
$\ZF$: if $S\subseteq(V_{\beta\cdot\omega})^{<\omega}$ carries a well-founded
relation, its rank function maps $S$ onto an ordinal $\xi$, and
$\zeta\mapsto\{s:\mathrm{rk}(s)=\zeta\}$ injects $\xi$ into
$\PP(S)\subseteq V_{\beta\cdot\omega+\omega+1}$, so $\xi<\hbar(\beta)$.

\begin{definition}[iteration]\label{def:It}
For a monotone $\Psi:D_\kappa\to D_\kappa$ and $\ell\in D_o$ define
$N_\xi$ for every ordinal $\xi$ by $N_0:=\bot$, the least element of
$D_\kappa$, $N_{\xi+1}:=N_\xi\sqcup\Psi(N_\xi)$, and
$N_\xi:=\bigsqcup_{\zeta<\xi}N_\zeta$ at limits; and
$\mathrm{It}(\Psi,\ell):=N_\ell$ if $\ell<\Theta$, and the top element
$\top$ of $D_\kappa$ if $\ell=\Theta$. It is monotone in both arguments, and
$N_\xi\le\top$ for all $\xi$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[profiles, continued]\label{def:P2}
Definition~\ref{def:P} is extended as follows; $\hat c(p)$ is the constant
function with value $p$, at the kind required.
\begin{itemize}
\item A small $W$-type has the profile of a small $\Sigma$-type;
  $\Acc_R\,x$ and $\mathsf{acc}\,x\,h$ have profile $1$.
\item $P_{\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d}(\pi):=P_a(\pi)\sqcup P_d(\pi)\cdot1$.
\item $P_{\ind^W_Cf}(\pi)$, for the $W$-type $Wx{:}A.B$: if
  $\Pi w{:}W.\,C$ is small it is $P_f(\pi)$; otherwise it is the function
  $p\mapsto\mathrm{It}(\Psi_p,\ell_p)$ on $D_{k(W)}$ with
  \[
  \ell_p:=\hbar\bigl(P_B(\pi[x{:=}p])\bigr),\qquad
  \Psi_p(n):=\bigl((P_f(\pi)\cdot p)\cdot\hat c(p)\bigr)\cdot\hat c(n),
  \]
  $\hbar$ being capped at $\Theta$.
\item $P_{\ind^{\Acc}_Cf}(\pi)$, for the carrier $X$: if
  $\Pi x{:}X.\,\Acc_R\,x\to C$ is small it is $P_f(\pi)$; otherwise it is
  the function constantly $\mathrm{It}(\Psi,\ell)$ in both arguments,
  with $\ell:=\hbar(P_X(\pi))$ and
  $\Psi(n):=(P_f(\pi)\cdot1)\cdot\hat c(\hat c(n))$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[fundamental lemma, continued]\label{lem:fund2}
Lemma~\ref{lem:fund} holds for $\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$, for every $\Theta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The new cases. \emph{Small $W$-types} as small $\Sigma$-types.
\emph{$\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d$:} the root label is $\lhd P_a$; a subtree is a
value $\sem d(b)$ at an index $b$ in the decoding of a fibre, which is a
small type, so $b\lhd_o1$ and the labels of $\sem d(b)$ are bounded by
$P_d\cdot1$.
\emph{$\ind^W_Cf$ at a small product:} the value at $w$ is
$F(a)(d)(\mathrm{rec}_w)$, a value of a value of a value of $F\lhd_oP_f$.
\emph{$\ind^W_Cf$ at a big product.} Let $w$ be a tree in the domain with
$w\lhd p$, and let $V(w)$ be the value at $w$. The fibre at a label $a$
of $w$ is $\sem B\rho[x{:=}a]$, or $\bot$ if the family was sanitized
there, and $\sem B\rho[x{:=}a]\lhd_o\beta_B:=P_B(\pi[x{:=}p])$ by induction,
since $a\lhd p$. If $\beta_B<\Theta$, each fibre is therefore a pure code
of rank below $\max(\beta_B,\omega)$ or is not a code, so the indices at
every node lie in $V_{\beta_B\cdot\omega}$, the nodes of $w$ are finite
sequences of such indices, and the height of $w$ is below
$\hbar(\beta_B)=\ell_p$, which is a limit ordinal. We show
$V(w')\lhd N_{\mathrm{ht}(w')+1}$ for every subtree $w'$ of $w$, by
induction on $w'=\mathsf{sup}\,a\,d$, where $N$ is the iteration of
$\Psi_p$. We have $a\lhd p$; the subtree function $d$ is
$\lhd\hat c(p)$, its values being subtrees of $w$; and $\mathrm{rec}_{w'}$,
which sends $b$ to $V(d(b))$, is $\lhd\hat c(N_{\mathrm{ht}(w')})$ by the
induction hypothesis and monotonicity of $N$ in its index. Since
$F\lhd P_f$, the value $F(a)(d)(\mathrm{rec}_{w'})$ is
$\lhd\Psi_p(N_{\mathrm{ht}(w')})\le N_{\mathrm{ht}(w')+1}$. So
$V(w)\lhd N_{\mathrm{ht}(w)+1}$, which is $\le N_{\ell_p}$ if
$\ell_p<\Theta$ and $\le\top$ in any case.
\emph{$\ind^{\Acc}_Cf$:} at a small product as for $W$. At a big product,
the carrier is the decoding of $c:=\sem X\rho\lhd_oP_X$, so it is a subset
of $V_{\beta_X\cdot\omega}$ with $\beta_X:=P_X(\pi)$, and the rank of an
accessible element in the relation $\prec$ is below $\hbar(\beta_X)=\ell$.
By induction along $\prec$, $V(x)\lhd N_{\mathrm{rk}(x)+1}\le\mathrm{It}(\Psi,\ell)$: the elements
of the carrier are $\lhd_o1$, $\mathrm{rec}_x$ sends $y\prec x$ to
$\star\mapsto V(y)$ and the other $y$ to the empty function, so it is
$\lhd\hat c(\hat c(N_{\mathrm{rk}(x)}))$, and $F\lhd P_f$. Outside the
accessible part the value is the empty function.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Correct ordinals in place of regular cardinals}

In Section~\ref{sec:proved} the bound stayed below a regular $\Theta$
because the only supremum was countable. Now $\ell_p$ is a Hartogs number
of a bound that is itself below $\Theta$, and an iteration of that length
stays below $\Theta$ for every monotone $\Psi$ only if $\Theta$ is
inaccessible. But the functions being iterated are not arbitrary: they
are denoted by terms. An ordinal that is elementary for definable
functions is closed under their iterations, whatever its cofinality.

Call $\alpha$ \emph{correct} if $V_\alpha\prec_{\Sigma_2}V$. Since satisfaction
for $\Sigma_2$ formulas is definable, this is a single formula, and by the
reflection theorem $\ZF$ proves that the correct ordinals are unbounded.
The level $2$ is what the argument needs, and it does not depend on the
term being bounded: elementarity will be applied to one formula only,
the denotation relation $v=\sem E^\Theta$ of the profile calculus defined
below, with the profile term $E$ as a parameter. That relation is
defined by recursion on $E$ over the sets $D^\Theta_\kappa$, which are
obtained from $\Theta$ by iterated monotone function spaces, and
$\hbar$ is a Hartogs number of some $V_\delta$; so it is $\Sigma_1$ in the
language with the power-set operation, hence $\Sigma_2$. We use the
following standard fact.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:correct}
Let $\alpha$ be correct, let $F$ be a class function that is
$\Sigma_2$-definable with parameters in $V_\alpha$, and let $a\in V_\alpha$ with
$a\subseteq\dom F$. Then $F[a]\in V_\alpha$. In particular $\alpha$ is a limit,
$\beta\cdot\omega<\alpha$ and $\hbar(\beta)<\alpha$ for $\beta<\alpha$, and the
domains $D^\Theta_\kappa$ for $\Theta<\alpha$ belong to $V_\alpha$ and are
computed correctly there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\varphi(x,y)$ define $F$. For $x\in a$ the sentence $\exists y\,\varphi(x,y)$
is $\Sigma_2$ and true, so it holds in $V_\alpha$, with a witness that is
$F(x)$ by upward absoluteness; so $F[a]\subseteq V_\alpha$. The sentence
$\exists b\,\forall x{\in}a\,\exists y{\in}b\,\varphi(x,y)$ is equivalent in
$\ZF$ to a $\Sigma_2$ sentence, by $\Sigma_2$-collection, and it is true by
Replacement; so it holds in $V_\alpha$, and $F[a]$ is included in an element
of $V_\alpha$.
\end{proof}

So a correct ordinal is regular \emph{for $\Sigma_2$-definable functions},
which is all the regularity the argument uses. We now write the
dependence on $\Theta$ explicitly: $D^\Theta_\kappa$, $\top^\Theta$, and
$P^\Theta_t$.

\paragraph{The profile calculus.}
The clauses of Definitions~\ref{def:P} and \ref{def:P2} are the
denotations of the terms of a simply kinded $\lambda$-calculus $\mathcal P$:
its kinds are those of Definition~\ref{def:kinds} with $T(\kappa)$, and its
constants are an ordinal constant $\bar\beta$ for each ordinal, the join
$\sqcup$ at each kind, the two operations
$(\beta,\beta')\mapsto\max(\beta\cdot\omega,\beta')+\omega$ and
$\beta\mapsto\beta\cdot\omega+\omega$, the constant-function operators
$\hat c$, the supremum of iterates over $\N$ used for $\ind^\N$,
$\mathrm{It}$ at each kind, and $\hbar$. So to each term $t$ of
$\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$ corresponds a term $t^\flat$ of $\mathcal P$, with a
variable for each variable of $t$, such that
$P^\Theta_t(\pi)=\sem{t^\flat}^\Theta(\pi)$ for every $\Theta$; the
denotation $\sem E^\Theta$ is defined by recursion on $E$, uniformly in
$\Theta$, an ordinal constant or an arithmetical result that is not below
$\Theta$ denoting the top element. A \emph{closure} is a closed term of
$\mathcal P$; it is \emph{below $\alpha$} if its ordinal constants are.

\begin{lemma}[complexity of the evaluator]\label{lem:complexity}
The relation $v=\sem E^\Theta_\eta$, in the variables $v$, $E$, $\Theta$, and
the assignment $\eta$, is $\Delta_1$ in the language with the power-set
operation, hence $\Delta_2$ in $\ZF$. So is the statement of
Lemma~\ref{lem:defsup} below, in the parameters $E$ and $\ell$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta_0^{\PP}$ for the formulas whose quantifiers are bounded by
terms built with $\PP$. The relation holds iff there are witnesses of
the following kinds satisfying a $\Delta_0^{\PP}$ condition, and the
witnesses are unique, which gives both a $\Sigma_1^{\PP}$ and a $\Pi_1^{\PP}$
form. (i) The finite family $(D^\Theta_\kappa)_\kappa$ for the kinds occurring in
$E$: $D_o$ is an interval of ordinals, $D_{\kappa\to\kappa'}$ is the set of
monotone functions in $\PP(D_\kappa\times D_{\kappa'})$, products are products.
(ii) For each occurrence of $\hbar$ at an argument $\beta<\Theta$, the set
$W=V_{\beta\cdot\omega+\omega+1}$, which is given by a recursion with $\PP$, and the
value $\min(\Theta,\hartogs(W))$, which is the least $\xi\le\Theta$ such that
$\xi=\Theta$ or no element of $\PP(\xi\times W)$ is an injection of $\xi$ into
$W$. (iii) The computation table, a function assigning to each subterm
of $E$ and each assignment to its free variables, the assignments being
finite tuples from the $D^\Theta_\kappa$, its value: variables, abstractions,
applications, pairs, joins and the arithmetical constants by the evident
clauses, ordinal arithmetic being computed inside $\Theta+1$, and
$\mathrm{It}(\Psi,\ell)$ for $\ell<\Theta$ by the sequence
$\langle N_\xi:\xi\le\ell\rangle$ of elements of $D^\Theta_\kappa$, the supremum of
iterates over $\N$ being the case $\ell=\omega$. A $\Delta_0^{\PP}$ formula is
$\Delta_2$ in $\ZF$: replace each term $\PP(y)$ by a variable $z$ subject to
$z=\PP(y)$, which is $\Pi_1$, quantified existentially or universally,
using Collection to pass the quantifier over $z$ through a bounded
quantifier. For the statement of Lemma~\ref{lem:defsup} add the witnesses
$\Theta$ and $s$ and, for the bounded quantifier over $\xi<\ell$, a function
supplying the witnesses for each $\xi$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[coherence]\label{lem:coh}
For $\Theta\le\Theta'$ define $p\sim_\kappa p'$ between $D^\Theta_\kappa$ and
$D^{\Theta'}_\kappa$: at $o$ and $T(\kappa)$ by recursion, $\beta\sim_o\beta'$ iff
$\beta=\Theta$ or $\beta=\beta'$; at $\kappa\to\kappa'$, $p\sim p'$ iff
$p(q)\sim p'(q')$ whenever $q\sim q'$; componentwise at products. Then
$\sem E^\Theta\sim\sem E^{\Theta'}$ for every closure $E$. In particular,
if $E$ has kind $o$ and $\sem E^\Theta<\Theta$ then
$\sem E^{\Theta'}=\sem E^\Theta$ for all $\Theta'\ge\Theta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The fundamental lemma of logical relations, for which it suffices that
the constants are related to themselves. The top element is related to
everything, by induction on the kind. For the arithmetical operations
and $\hbar$: if the arguments on the left are below $\Theta$ they equal
those on the right, and the result on the left is the true result or the
top. For a supremum of a family: if some member on the left is the top
so is the supremum; otherwise the members agree with those on the right,
and the supremum on the left is theirs if it is below $\Theta$ and the top
if not; at higher kinds pointwise. For $\mathrm{It}$: if
$\ell=\Theta$ on the left the value is the top; otherwise
$\ell=\ell'$, and $N_\xi\sim N'_\xi$ by induction on $\xi\le\ell$, from the
cases of join and supremum and $\Psi\sim\Psi'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[definably proper]\label{def:Q}
Let $\alpha$ be correct. $\mathcal Q_\kappa\subseteq D^\alpha_\kappa$ is defined by
recursion on $\kappa$: $p\in\mathcal Q_\kappa$ iff $p=\sem E^\alpha$ for some
closure $E$ below $\alpha$, and: at $o$, $p<\alpha$; at $\kappa'\to\kappa''$,
$p(q)\in\mathcal Q_{\kappa''}$ for every $q\in\mathcal Q_{\kappa'}$; at
products, componentwise; and $\mathcal Q_{T(\kappa')}:=\mathcal Q_{\kappa'}$.
Both $D^\alpha_\kappa$ and the closures below $\alpha$ form sets,
so $\mathcal Q$ is defined in $\ZF$ by a single recursion on kinds.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[definable suprema]\label{lem:defsup}
Let $\alpha$ be correct, $\ell<\alpha$, and let $E(\cdot)$ be a term of
$\mathcal P$ of kind $o$ with one ordinal constant left open, otherwise
closed and below $\alpha$. If $\sem{E(\bar\xi)}^\alpha<\alpha$ for every
$\xi<\ell$, then $\sup_{\xi<\ell}\sem{E(\bar\xi)}^\alpha<\alpha$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider the statement: there are $\Theta$ and a function $s$ on $\ell$
with $s(\xi)=\sem{E(\bar\xi)}^\Theta<\Theta$ for all $\xi<\ell$. It is
$\Sigma_2$ in the parameters $E$ and $\ell$, which lie in $V_\alpha$, and
it holds in $V$ with $\Theta:=\alpha$. So it holds in $V_\alpha$, with some
$\Theta''<\alpha$ and $s''\in V_\alpha$, the denotation being computed
correctly in $V_\alpha$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:coh},
$s''(\xi)=\sem{E(\bar\xi)}^{\Theta''}=\sem{E(\bar\xi)}^\alpha$. So the
supremum is at most $\Theta''<\alpha$.
\end{proof}

This is the whole point: the sequence of iterates is a definable set, an
elementary $V_\alpha$ contains it, and the cofinality of $\alpha$ never
enters. The least correct ordinal has cofinality $\omega$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Q}
Let $\alpha$ be correct. Every closure below $\alpha$ denotes, in
$D^\alpha$, an element of $\mathcal Q$; more generally a term of
$\mathcal P$ below $\alpha$ denotes an element of $\mathcal Q$ under every
assignment of elements of $\mathcal Q$ to its variables.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the term, as in Tait's method; an assignment in
$\mathcal Q$ can be replaced by a substitution of closures, so every
denotation that arises is that of a closure below $\alpha$. It remains to
see that the constants are in $\mathcal Q$. An ordinal constant below
$\alpha$ is. The arithmetical operations and $\hbar$ map ordinals below
$\alpha$ to ordinals below $\alpha$, since $\alpha$ is correct. Joins and
constant functions are immediate. For the supremum of iterates and for
$\mathrm{It}(\Psi,\ell)$ with $\Psi\in\mathcal Q$ and $\ell<\alpha$: by
induction on $\xi\le\ell$ we show $N_\xi\in\mathcal Q$. It is the
denotation of the closure $\mathrm{It}(\bar\Psi,\bar\xi)$. At a successor,
$N_{\xi+1}=N_\xi\sqcup\Psi(N_\xi)$ with $\Psi,N_\xi\in\mathcal Q$. At a
limit, apply $N_\xi$ to arguments in $\mathcal Q$ until the kind is $o$;
the result is $\sup_{\zeta<\xi}\sem{E(\bar\zeta)}^\alpha$ for the term
$E(\bar\zeta)$ that applies $\mathrm{It}(\bar\Psi,\bar\zeta)$ to closures
denoting those arguments, each value is below $\alpha$ by the induction
hypothesis, and Lemma~\ref{lem:defsup} applies. The supremum over $\N$ is
the case $\ell=\omega$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cic2}
$(\ZF)$ Let $\alpha$ be correct. For every triple $(\Gamma,t,\theta)$ of
$\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$ with $t$ of kind $o$ and every $\gamma<\alpha$ there is
$\delta<\alpha$, computed from $(t,\theta,\gamma)$ by a recursion on syntax,
such that in every structure, under every oracle, and at every
$\gamma$-generated environment $\rho$ for $(\Gamma,\theta)$, if
$\sem t^\X\rho$ is a code of infinite rank then its rank is below $\delta$.
In particular $\alpha$ is $0$-closed, so $\lambda_0$ exists.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
As for Theorem~\ref{thm:rankcic2}, with $\Theta:=\alpha$. The profiles
$\pi_\theta(x)$, which are $\hat\gamma$ at the free variables and
$P^\alpha_{t_x}(\pi_{\theta_x})$ at the others, are denotations of closures
below $\alpha$ and lie in $\mathcal Q$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Q}, and so does
$\delta:=P^\alpha_t(\pi_\theta)$; so $\delta<\alpha$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:fund2}, which holds for every $\Theta$ and every structure,
$\sem t^\X\rho\lhd_o\delta$. The function $(t,\theta,\gamma)\mapsto\delta$ is
defined, and the argument carried out, uniformly in the triple: the
induction in Lemma~\ref{lem:Q} is an induction on terms inside $\ZF$,
about the sets $\mathcal Q_\kappa$.
\end{proof}

The uniformity deserves emphasis, because it is the difference between
proving the consistency of $\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM$ and proving the
consistency of each of its finite fragments. A proof that bounded each
term by a separate appeal to Replacement would give only the latter.
Here the appeal to Replacement is made once, in the reflection theorem
that produces a correct $\alpha$ for a fixed $m$; after that every term is
handled by one induction about sets in $V_{\alpha+\omega}$.

\begin{remark}[why the logical complexity of the type theory does not enter]\label{rem:complexity}
$\CIC_2$ expresses propositions of unbounded complexity, by quantifying
over $\Type_0$ and over the types built on it, and one may ask why a
fixed level of correctness can suffice. It suffices because elementarity
is never applied to anything in which a proposition of the type theory
occurs. (1) The fundamental lemma is proved in the universe, for every
structure and every oracle, and the profile of a term is computed without
evaluating a proposition: every proposition has profile $1$. A subset
type $\Sigma h{:}\varphi.\,B$ has the bound of $B$ whatever $\varphi$ is, a
quotient has the bound of its carrier whatever the relation is, and an
$\Acc$-recursion has the height bound of its carrier whatever the
relation is. (2) Truth in the model is computed in the universe and is
not reflected; and since every code of $\CIC_2$ lies in $V_{\alpha+\omega}$
the model is a set, so truth in it is an ordinary definition by recursion
and soundness is one induction on derivations inside $\ZF$. (3) A
proposition can decide how far a recursion runs: by $\Acc$-recursion on
$\N$ along $R\,m\,n:=m=n+1\wedge\varphi(n)$, the family
$T(n):=\Sigma h{:}\varphi(n).\,T(n+1)$ searches for the first failure of an
arbitrary $\varphi$. It cannot raise the bound on the height, because no term
turns a proposition into data: there is no description operator and no
elimination of truncation into types. This is where the hypothesis on
the theory is used, and it is used at one identifiable place: if a
description operator is added, the fundamental lemma has no clause for
it at the type $\Type_0$, since truth would then select a code of any
rank below the cut; and with a description operator the theory validates
Replacement and does need inaccessibles. So the single formula to which
elementarity is applied is the evaluator of the profile calculus, which
is ordinal arithmetic built from $\beta\cdot\omega+\omega$, $\hbar$, suprema over
$\N$, and iteration.

Because the level is fixed, Theorem~\ref{thm:cic2} is one theorem of
$\ZF$ and not a schema, and with the soundness lemma it yields the single
sentence $\Con(\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM)$; the comparison with $\ZF$ is then
strict by the second incompleteness theorem. Had the level needed to grow
with the term, the argument would have shown only that $\ZF$ proves the
consistency of each finite fragment, which $\ZF$ proves of itself and
which is compatible with equal strength. In fact the argument uses only
the existence of one $\Sigma_2$-correct ordinal and recursions of length
below $\alpha+\omega$, so it should go through in a fragment of $\ZF$ with
Replacement restricted to a low level of complexity; we have not
determined which.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:cic2}
With Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, specialised to one cut and to oracles
indexed by kind, $\ZF\vdash\Con(\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM)$, subject to the
rule-by-rule check of Lemma~\ref{lem:sound}. The interpretation of
$\ZFh$ in \cite{paper2} is written for the extensional theory; it uses
only $W$-types with small branching and $\Acc$ on small carriers, and
equality only through rewriting, so we expect it to go through in
$\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM$, which has function and propositional
extensionality. Granting both, the consistency strength of
$\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM$ lies strictly between that of $\ZFh$ and that of
$\ZF$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{What is excluded, and why.}
$W$-types with big branching and $\Acc$ on a big carrier. For these the
full height of a recursion is not bounded by the bounds of its data: an
oracle, or a parameter of content $0$, can supply a well-order of the
universe of any length, and a tree with branching over the universe of
any height. The value of a recursion is small all the same, because a
step can call the recursive function only at the values of finitely
many index terms, in environments varying over small types; but a pure
profile cannot see this. We tried three ways to make it see it, and
record why each fails. \emph{Restricting the height to the predecessors
bounded by a profile $q$:} the step may compute an index from the value
of a recursive call, so $q$ must dominate the bounds being defined, and
$q\ge N_{\hbar(q)}$ has no solution when the step raises ranks.
\emph{A pre-fixed point of the step's profile:} it bounds the recursion
whatever the height, but $n\mapsto n+\omega$ has no proper pre-fixed point
while the true bound is a countable ordinal. \emph{Splitting the
occurrences of the recursive function into those shielded by a
parameter, whose value is bounded whatever it is given, and the direct
ones, and replacing the function by junk outside the directly called
indices:} whether an occurrence is shielded can depend on data, as in
$(\lambda\varphi.\,\varphi\,(\lambda G.\,\mathrm{rec}\,(h\,G)))\,(\ind^{\mathbf 2}\,K\,(\lambda\psi.\,\psi\,g_0)\,b)$,
and the directly called indices of a function that is passed around
unapplied form a family indexed by its future arguments. What is wanted
is a relation that bounds a value either by a constant or by the values
of the recursive function on a small set, hereditarily; we have not
found its definition. General inductive families with big indices or big
recursive arguments meet the same problem. Section~\ref{sec:bigacc}
argues that for $\Acc$ on a big carrier no such relation exists, because
the bound it would establish is false.

\section{Accessibility on a big carrier gives Replacement}\label{sec:bigacc}

This section is a lower bound, and it goes against
Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}. It was found after the rest of the paper
was written, by asking why the first failed attempt above fails; it has
been checked once and read by nobody else. The claim is that in
$\CIC_2+\EM$ with $\Acc$ on the big carrier of sets-as-trees, an ordinal
that is merely \emph{specified} by a proposition can be \emph{computed}
as an element, which is the instance of unique choice that Replacement
needs. The mechanism is the transfinite form of the well-known
extraction of a witness from $\exists n.\,P\,n$ by recursion on an
accessibility proof. Two things replace the decidability of $P$ that the
extraction needs: a set can be guarded by a proposition, and a step of
the recursion can index its recursive calls by the \emph{value} of an
earlier recursive call.

\paragraph{Sets.}
Let $V:=W X{:}\Type_0.\,X:\Type_1$ with Aczel's bisimulation $\approx$ and
membership, both in $\Prop$ \cite{paper2}. We use the following closed
terms, all definable with $W$-recursion and small sums: $\emptyset$; the
pair $\langle p,v\rangle$; $\mathrm{succ}\,u=u\cup\{u\}$; the union
$\bigcup_{i:\bar u}t(i)$ of a family indexed by the index type $\bar u$ of a
set $u=\mathsf{sup}\,\bar u\,u_\bullet$; the \emph{guard}
$\{t\mid P\}:=\mathsf{sup}\,P\,t$ for $P:\Prop$ and $t:P\to V$, whose union
$\bigcup\{t\mid P\}$ is $t$ if $P$ holds and $\emptyset$ if not; and
$D(u)$, the set of well-orderings of subsets of $u\cup\omega$, obtained by
separation from a power set. Ordinals are von Neumann ordinals in $V$.
In $V$, provably in the type theory, any two well-orderings are
comparable, so ``$w$ has order type $\xi$'' and ``$w$ has order type below
$\eta$'' are propositions with the expected properties, and the order
types of the elements of $D(u)$ are exactly the ordinals below the
Hartogs number $\hartogs(u\cup\omega)$.

\begin{definition}[the materializing recursion]\label{def:mat}
Fix a parameter $u:V$. For $G:V$ let
$B_u(G):=\{0\}\cup\{\langle 1,w\rangle: w\in D(G)\}\cup\{\langle 2,a\rangle:a\in u\}$,
a set of \emph{labels}. A \emph{path} is an element of $V$ read as a
nested pair: $\emptyset$ is the root, and $\langle p,v\rangle$ is the child of
$p$ with label $v$. For $R:V\to V\to\Prop$ define the step
$f_R:\Pi p{:}V.\,(\Pi c{:}V.\,R\,c\,p\to V)\to V$ by
\begin{align*}
G&:=\textstyle\bigcup\{\mathrm{rec}\,\langle p,0\rangle\,h\mid h:R\,\langle p,0\rangle\,p\},\\
f_R\,p\,\mathrm{rec}&:=\textstyle\bigcup_{v\in B_u(G)}\ \bigcup\{\mathrm{succ}\,(\mathrm{rec}\,\langle p,v\rangle\,h)\mid h:R\,\langle p,v\rangle\,p\},
\end{align*}
and let $F_R:=\ind^{\Acc}f_R:\Pi p{:}V.\,\Acc_R\,p\to V$. The carrier $V$
is big and the motive is the constant $V$. The step first calls the
recursion at the child with label $0$, and then at the children whose
labels are computed from the value $G$ of that call; every call is
guarded, because the step has no proof that the child is below $p$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[coherent assignments]\label{def:coherent}
A \emph{target assignment} is a relation $\tau:V\to V\to\Prop$ that
respects $\approx$ and is a partial function from paths to ordinals; we
write $\tau(p)$ for the value. Put $G_\tau(p):=\tau(\langle p,0\rangle)$ if
that is defined and $\emptyset$ otherwise. The assignment is
\emph{coherent} if
\begin{enumerate}
\item whenever $\tau(\langle p,v\rangle)$ and $\tau(p)$ are both defined,
  $\tau(\langle p,v\rangle)<\tau(p)$ (a path with a target may have a parent
  without one; this is used at the root in Theorem~\ref{thm:bigacc}); and
\item whenever $\tau(p)$ is defined,
  $\tau(p)=\sup\{\tau(\langle p,v\rangle)+1: v\in B_u(G_\tau(p)),\ \tau(\langle p,v\rangle)\text{ defined}\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Its relation is $R_\tau\,c\,p:\Longleftrightarrow\exists v.\ c\approx\langle p,v\rangle\wedge\tau(c)\text{ is defined}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[materialization]\label{lem:mat}
$\CIC_2+\EM$ proves: if $\tau$ is coherent and $\tau(p)$ is defined, then
$p$ is $R_\tau$-accessible, and $F_{R_\tau}\,p\,a\approx\tau(p)$ for every
$a:\Acc_{R_\tau}\,p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By induction on the ordinal $\tau(p)$, which is $\in$-induction on $V$ into
$\Prop$. If $R_\tau\,c\,p$ then $\tau(c)$ is defined and, by (1) and
invariance under $\approx$, below $\tau(p)$; so $c$ is accessible by the
induction hypothesis, and $p$ is accessible by the constructor. For the
value, $F\,p\,a$ is propositionally $f\,p\,(\lambda c\,h.\,F\,c\,(\cdots))$. By
the induction hypothesis every recursive call whose guard holds returns
the target of its child. So the set $G$ of the step is
$\approx G_\tau(p)$, hence $B_u(G)\approx B_u(G_\tau(p))$, the guard at the label
$v$ holds iff $\tau(\langle p,v\rangle)$ is defined, and the value is the
union of the successors of those targets, which is their strict
supremum, which is $\tau(p)$ by (2).
\end{proof}

A coherent assignment is a proposition; the lemma turns its root target
into the value of a term. The height of the recursion is not bounded by
anything computed from the root: a node whose child $0$ has target
$\zeta$ has children indexed by the well-orderings of $\zeta$, whose
targets range up to the Hartogs number of $\zeta$, and so on downwards.
This is the dependence of an index on the value of a recursive call that
defeated the restriction of heights in Section~\ref{sec:cic2}.

\paragraph{Canonical assignments.}
Say that an ordinal $\eta>0$ is of \emph{type 1} if
$\eta\le\hartogs(\zeta\cup\omega)$ for some $\zeta<\eta$, and let $\zeta(\eta)$ be
the least such; every $\eta\le\omega_1$ is of type 1 with $\zeta(\eta)=0$, and
an ordinal fails to be of type 1 exactly if it is above $\omega_1$ and
closed under $\zeta\mapsto\hartogs(\zeta)$. The
clauses for a node $p$ with target $\eta$ of type 1 are:
$\tau(\langle p,0\rangle):=\zeta(\eta)$, and
$\tau(\langle p,\langle 1,w\rangle\rangle):=$ the order type of $w$, if $w$ is a
well-ordering of order type below $\eta$. Conditions (1) and (2) hold at
$p$: the labels $\langle 1,w\rangle$ with $w\in D(\zeta(\eta))$ have as order
types all the ordinals below $\hartogs(\zeta(\eta)\cup\omega)\ge\eta$. If $\eta$
is not of type 1 and $c:\mu\to\eta$ is cofinal and increasing with
$\mu<\eta$, the clauses are $\tau(\langle p,0\rangle):=\mu$ and
$\tau(\langle p,\langle 1,w\rangle\rangle):=c(\xi)$ for $w$ of order type
$\xi<\mu$. In both cases the children with a target have targets
cofinal in $\eta$ (all ordinals below $\eta$, or the values of $c$), and
the child $0$ has a target below $\eta$, so (1) and (2) hold at $p$ as soon
as every child with a target has itself a subtree with that target.
These clauses define $\tau$ on a path by recursion on its length, with no
reference to $F$, as soon as a cofinal map is chosen definably for every
ordinal that is not of type 1 (Lemma~\ref{lem:rule} below makes this
precise). We know of no way to do that in $V$ without choice, and some
choice of a canonical map is unavoidable: an ordinal is the target of
a node only if a definable rule sends labels, which are sets of rank below
it, cofinally into it. So we take the maps from the constructible sets of
$V$: $c_\eta$ is the $<_L$-least increasing cofinal map in $L$ from the least
ordinal $\mu_\eta$ from which $L$ has one. A cofinal map in $L$ is cofinal in
$V$. Call $\eta$ \emph{handled} if $\eta=0$ (no child has a target), or
$\eta$ is of type 1, or $\mu_\eta<\eta$.

\begin{lemma}[canonical assignments from a local rule]\label{lem:rule}
Let $r(\eta,v,\xi)$ be a formula, the \emph{rule}, which for each $\eta$
and label $v$ determines at most one $\xi$, respects $\approx$, and satisfies,
for every $\eta$ in a class $C$: every $\xi$ with $r(\eta,v,\xi)$ is in $\eta\cap C$;
and $\eta=\bigcup\{\xi+1: r(\eta,v,\xi),\ v\in B_u(G)\}$, where $G$ is the $\xi$
with $r(\eta,0,\xi)$, or $\emptyset$. Let $\tau_\eta$ be defined by recursion on
the length of paths: $\tau_\eta(\emptyset)=\eta$, and $\tau_\eta(\langle p,v\rangle)=\xi$
iff $r(\tau_\eta(p),v,\xi)$. Then for $\eta\in C$ the assignment $\tau_\eta$ is
coherent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every target is in $C$, by induction on the path. Condition (1) is the
first hypothesis on $r$, and (2) at $p$ is the second, applied to
$\tau_\eta(p)\in C$, because the targets of the children of $p$ are the
$\xi$ with $r(\tau_\eta(p),v,\xi)$ and $G_{\tau_\eta}(p)$ is the $G$ of the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

With the rule of the clauses above, the handled ordinals $\eta$ such that
every ordinal below $\eta$ is handled form a class $C$ satisfying the
hypotheses. An ordinal $\eta>\omega$ that is not handled is a limit
cardinal of $L$, since $(|\zeta|^+)^L\le\hartogs(\zeta)$ (an ordinal that
injects into $\zeta$ in $L$ injects into it in $V$), and it is regular in
$L$, because $\mu_\eta=\eta$. Let $\rho$ be the least such ordinal, if there
is one. Then $C$ is the class of all ordinals below $\rho$, or of all
ordinals if there is no $\rho$.

\begin{theorem}[claimed]\label{thm:bigacc}
$\ZFC$ is interpretable in $\CIC_2+\EM$: either the sets-as-trees satisfy
$\ZF$, or $L_\rho$ satisfies $\ZFC$ for an inaccessible cardinal $\rho$ of $L$.
Consequently
$\Con(\CIC_2+\EM)$ implies $\Con(\ZF)$, provably in primitive recursive
arithmetic, and $\ZF$ does not prove $\Con(\CIC_2+\EM)$ unless it is
inconsistent. The same holds for any extension, in particular for
$\CIC_\omega+\EM$, so Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} fails for the theory as
stated in Section~\ref{sec:tt}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Work in the type theory, reasoning in $\Prop$ about $V$, which satisfies
Zermelo set theory with $\in$-recursion \cite{paper2}; in particular
$V_\alpha$ exists for every ordinal $\alpha$, by recursion on $\alpha$, and so do
$L$ and its canonical well-order. By excluded middle either $\rho$ exists
or not. If it exists, $L_\rho$ satisfies $\ZFC$, by the usual argument for
an inaccessible of $L$: Replacement because $\rho$ is regular in $L$ and a
function definable over $L_\rho$ is in $L$, Power set and Separation
because a constructible subset of $x\in L_\rho$ is in $L_{(|x|^+)^L}$ by
condensation. If not, we show that $V$ satisfies Replacement; then $V$
satisfies $\ZF$, and $L$ satisfies $\ZFC$ by G\"odel's argument inside
$V$. Let $s$ be a set and let $\varphi$ define a function on $s$, with
$\eta_a$ the rank of the value at $a\in s$. Take $u:=s$ in
Definition~\ref{def:mat}, and
let $\tau(\langle\emptyset,\langle 2,a\rangle\rangle):=\eta_a$, and below the path
$\langle\emptyset,\langle 2,a\rangle\rangle$ the canonical assignment
$\tau_{\eta_a}$. The root is given \emph{no} target: its target would be
$\sup_a(\eta_a+1)$, whose existence is the point at issue. (An earlier
version of this sketch assigned it, which is circular; the
formalization found this.) The assignment is coherent, and it is a
single proposition, defined from $s$ and $\varphi$ without any choice of one
assignment for each $a$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mat} every child of the root
that has a target is accessible, so the root is accessible by the
constructor, and unfolding the recursion once shows that the root value
$F_{R_\tau}\,\emptyset\,a$, which is a term of type $V$ because $s$ is a
variable of type $V$, is the union of the $\eta_a+1$. So there is a set
$\theta$ with $\eta_a<\theta$ for all $a\in s$, and the image of $s$ is
separated from $V_\theta$. No reflection argument is needed, because
Replacement is proved for $V$ itself and not for $L$. The interpretation
of $\ZFC$ relativizes to $L_\rho$ if $\rho$ exists and to $L$ otherwise,
which is one definable class.
\end{proof}

The case distinction is only needed because we cannot choose cofinal
maps in $V$. Note that the argument uses $L$ only for its definable
well-order: any definable class with a definable well-order that
computes the cofinalities that matter would do. If $V$ has a definable
well-order and no inaccessible cardinal, taking the maps from $V$
itself shows that the sets-as-trees satisfy $\ZF$, with no reference
to $L$.

\paragraph{What has and has not been checked.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:mat} and the gluing step of Theorem~\ref{thm:bigacc} are
formalized in Lean~4 without Mathlib (directory \texttt{lean/}): the
theorem \texttt{PSet.materialize} is the lemma, for paths that are lists
of labels, targets that are arbitrary sets, and an arbitrary operation
in place of $D$; and \texttt{PSet.replacement} says that a functional
relation on a set whose values lie in a class with uniformly defined
coherent assignments has an image. Both depend on the axiom
\texttt{propext} only: no choice, no quotients, and no excluded middle.
What is not formalized is that the handled ordinals form such a
class. The parts taken on
trust are the theory of $L$ inside $V$, that is, in Zermelo set theory
with $\in$-recursion (the canonical well-order, and condensation for the
power set axiom in $L_\rho$), and
the intensional form of the interpretation of \cite{paper2}. The second
universe is used only to form $V$. With $n+2$ universes we expect, and have
not checked, that the rank of the sets of one level is a regular, hence
inaccessible, cardinal of the next, by the same recursion with a
parameter from the higher level in the relation.

\paragraph{Consequences for this paper.}
(i) The restriction to small carriers in $\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$ is not a
limitation of the method. If Theorem~\ref{thm:cic2} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:bigacc} are both right, the line between theories that
$\ZF$ proves consistent and theories that interpret $\ZF$ runs between
$\Acc$ on small carriers and $\Acc$ on big ones. The recursion of
Definition~\ref{def:mat} cannot be run on a small carrier, because the
paths must be elements of the carrier and their labels range over sets
of unbounded rank; on a carrier $X$ the heights are below $\hbar$ of the
rank of $X$, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:fund2}.
(ii) Conjecture~\ref{conj:rank} is refuted for the full theory: with
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} it would give Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}. The
profile of $F_R$ is the least pre-fixed point of an operator that raises
ranks, which is the top element, and rightly so.
(iii) The model should be unsound for the full theory at the least
correct ordinal $\alpha$, concretely, when the ambient universe is $L$ and
has no inaccessible cardinal, as we may assume: the ordinals $\theta_n$
least with
$L_{\theta_n}\prec_{\Sigma_n}L_\alpha$ are defined by propositions, the term
$\lambda n.\,F\,\langle\emptyset,\langle 2,n\rangle\rangle$ materializes them, and
the $W$-recursion $T(\mathsf{sup}\,X\,g):=\Sigma x{:}X.\,T(g\,x)$ turns their
supremum into a small type of rank $\alpha$.
(iv) A choice or description operator is therefore not what gives the
type theory the strength of Replacement; large elimination of
accessibility over a type as big as the motive is enough. The question
of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} remains for the theory in which $\Acc$ is
taken on carriers in $\Type_0$ only, with any number of universes.

\section{Examples}\label{sec:examples}

\begin{example}[a climbing function]\label{ex:climb}
Suppose $\lambda_0$ has cofinality $\omega$; fix a cofinal sequence
$(\beta_n)$, let $\mathrm{next}(\beta)$ be the least $\beta_n>\beta$, and
let $F(X)$ be a code of rank $\mathrm{next}(\rk X)$. Every pointwise
condition on $F$, such as a Howard majorant mapping ordinals below
$\lambda_0$ to ordinals below $\lambda_0$, accepts $F$; yet
$\Sigma n{:}\N.\,F^n(\N):\Type_0$ denotes a code of rank $\lambda_0$. The
extent of a universe below an inaccessible is singular, so some small
index type always admits a cofinal family, and no condition on values
excludes it. In the model $F$ is excluded because it is not generated:
if $F=\sem s^\X\rho$ with $\rho$ of content below $\gamma<\lambda_0$ then
the term $\ind^\N\,\N\,(\lambda\_.\,s)\,x$, with $x{:}\N$ free, takes the
values $F^n(\N)$, which lie in $U_0$ and are cofinal in $\lambda_0$,
contradicting closedness.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[trees and Hartogs numbers]\label{ex:set}
Let $\mathsf{set}:=Wx{:}\Type_0.\,x$ and define $T:\mathsf{set}\to\Type_0$ by
$W$-recursion, $T(\mathsf{sup}\,X\,d):=(\Sigma x{:}X.\,T(d\,x))\to\Prop$. Then
$\rk T(w)$ is at least the height of $w$. A tree in the decoding under
the oracle $V_\vartheta$ over a label of rank below $\gamma$ can have any
height below a Hartogs number of $V_{\gamma\cdot\omega}$, and $T(w)$ with
$w$ free of content below $\gamma$ is then a type of that rank; so an
$N$-closed ordinal is closed under $\beta\mapsto\hartogs(V_{\beta+1})$, and
in $\ZFC$ it is a strong limit cardinal. In the model the subtree
functions are generated, and the heights of the trees of the model are
bounded below $\lambda_0$ for that reason.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[heterogeneous fibres]\label{ex:hetero}
Let $B'(0):=\Type_5$ and $B'(1):=\Type_0$. A family
$f:\N\to\Pi b{:}\mathbf 2.\,B'(b)$ can have ranks at $1$ cofinal in
$\lambda_0$ while being dominated, in any order that joins the fibres of
a dependent function, by a constant family that is large at $0$. Content
is computed fibre by fibre for this reason, and so must profiles be.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[why closedness is stated in the structure cut at $\lambda$ itself]\label{ex:sat}
A tempting alternative defines $\lambda_N$ as the least ordinal closed
under functions $\nu_t(\alpha)$ computed in the structure cut at
$\alpha<\lambda$, and compares the model with those structures
afterwards. It fails. In a structure cut at $\alpha$ the recursive
function of a recursion, as a bound variable of the step, ranges over
functions into the cut universe, so the computed bound of any
rank-raising recursion saturates near $\alpha$, every $\nu_t(\alpha)$ is
about $\alpha$, and the least closed ordinal is just the least ordinal
$\eta$ closed under $\alpha\mapsto\hartogs(V_{\alpha+1})$. But for
$X:\Type_N$ and a well-founded linear order $o=(R,\dots)$ on $X$ define
$T_o:X\to\Type_N$ by $\Acc$-recursion,
$T_o(x):=(\Sigma y{:}X.\,\Sigma h{:}R\,y\,x.\,T_o(y))\to\Prop$, and put
$t(X):=\Sigma o.\,\Sigma x{:}X.\,T_o(x)$. Then $\rk t(X)\ge\hartogs(\El(X))$,
the decoding of $T_o(x)$ is a cumulative hierarchy of height the order
type of $x$, the ranks of $t^n(\N)$ are cofinal in $\eta$, and
$\Sigma n{:}\N.\,t^n(\N):\Type_N$ has rank $\eta$. Under
Definition~\ref{def:closedord} the values $t^n(\N)$ are honest in the
structure cut at $\lambda$ as long as they lie below $\lambda$, so
$\lambda_N>\eta$; and no term can diagonalize against $\lambda_N$,
because the sequence in Lemma~\ref{lem:closedex} uses the bounds of all
terms at once.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[why the eliminator of equality checks the equation]\label{ex:confusion}
Closedness quantifies over oracles and environments that are not those
of the model, and at such an environment a proposition may be true for
no good reason. Let $B$ be a parameter with $B(f)=\code{\U_{N+1}}$ and
$B(g)=\code{\U_N}$ for two elements $f,g$, in a context with
$e:B\,f=B\,g$ and $Y:B\,f$. If $\ind^=$ evaluated to the identity
whenever a proof of the equation is supplied, the transport of $Y$ along
$e$ would be a term of type $\Type_N$ whose value is an arbitrary code of
$U_{N+1}$, of rank unbounded in the higher extents, and no ordinal would
be closed. Here the truth value of $B\,f=B\,g$ is computed, it is
$\bot$, and the context has no environment at all; and in general the
check makes the value junk unless the two sides are equal. In the
extensional theory the same confusion arises from reflection with no
eliminator to guard, which is one reason this paper is about the
intensional theory. Sanitized families answer the remaining case, a
well-typed family that takes a value outside its universe at an
argument outside the model.
\end{example}

\section{Status}\label{sec:status}

\emph{Proved, given the definitions:} the existence of closed ordinals
from the rank lemma (Lemma~\ref{lem:closedex}); the closure properties of
the generated elements (Lemma~\ref{lem:gen}); type formation
(Lemma~\ref{lem:tyform}). \emph{Proved by cases, to be checked rule by
rule:} soundness (Lemma~\ref{lem:sound}); the loose ends are the three
points listed after Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The design has been
attacked once on its own terms, case by case through the soundness
proof and with adversarial oracles against closedness; this produced
the clause that the types of a universe are hereditarily admitted
(without it a tree over a type with an ungenerated family need not be
generated) and nothing else.
\emph{Proved in special cases:} the rank lemma for $\CIC_2$ without $W$
and $\Acc$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rankcic2}), with $\omega_1$ as the extent of
$\Type_0$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:omega1}); and the existence of a closed
ordinal for $\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}$, the theory with two universes,
$W$-types of small branching and $\Acc$ on small carriers
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cic2}), the extent of $\Type_0$ being a
$\Sigma_2$-correct ordinal. Both proofs assume the standard metatheory of
the intensional theory, in Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}. The second has been
written once and attacked once, on the question of why a fixed level of
correctness suffices (Remark~\ref{rem:complexity}); the complexity of the
evaluator of the profile calculus is now written out
(Lemma~\ref{lem:complexity}), and the height bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fund2} has been rechecked, including fibres that are
sanitized or of finite rank. It has not been read by anyone else, and
the record of this project, in which every earlier design of the bound
failed on inspection, is the reason to want an independent check or a
formalization before relying on it.
\emph{Conditional consequence:} $\ZF\vdash\Con(\CIC_2^{\mathrm s}+\EM)$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:cic2}), given the rule-by-rule check of soundness; and
the placement of that theory strictly between $\ZFh$ and $\ZF$, given
also that the interpretation of \cite{paper2} goes through
intensionally.
\emph{Claimed, against the main conjecture:} $\Acc$ on a big carrier
interprets $\ZFC$ already with two universes
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bigacc}); the core, Lemma~\ref{lem:mat}, is written
out, and the theory of $L$ inside the sets-as-trees is taken on trust.
If it stands, Conjectures~\ref{conj:main} and~\ref{conj:rank} are false
for the theory of Section~\ref{sec:tt} and should be restated for
$\Acc$ on carriers in $\Type_0$.
\emph{Open:} $W$ with big branching, where no analogue of
Section~\ref{sec:bigacc} is known to us (a tree can have branching
types guarded by propositions, but we see no term that builds one of
specified height without accessibility); and more than two universes with
small carriers, where kinds are no longer syntactic (end of
Section~\ref{sec:proved}).
\emph{Not attempted:} general inductive families; the refinement to
finitely many universes, $\ZFh_{n+1}\vdash\Con(\CIC_{n+2}+\EM)$, which
would close the hierarchy of \cite{paper2}.

This paper supersedes \cite[\S8]{paper2}. That section went through
three designs: Howard-style majorants with closure functions computed in
cut structures and a retraction between structures, which fails by
Example~\ref{ex:sat}; a partial equivalence relation over full function
spaces for the extensional theory, with typed closedness under arbitrary
truth assignments, which is unsatisfiable by the extensional form of
Example~\ref{ex:confusion}; and the same with closedness as a gap
condition on untyped terms, for which the rank lemma is plausible but
resists proof because untyped terms include junk self-applications that
are typable under reflection. The present design keeps from them the
codes, the notion of content, the gap form of closedness, and the
examples, and replaces truth assignments and the partial equivalence
relation by the oracle.
